# The Žižekian core argument: formal verification and ideological fetishism

**Project:** *In Lean We Trust? The Ideology of Machine-Checked Proofs in Science*  
**Date:** 27 November 2026  
**Status:** concise statement of the controlling argument. The full theoretical and operational derivation is in THEORETICAL_MODEL.md; primary-text locators are in PRIMARY_TEXT_READING_AND_ARGUMENT.md.

## 1. Thesis

Lean's kernel provides a genuine, bounded form of formal assurance: it checks a proof term against a specified formal proposition under a specified environment. The paper asks how this real technical warrant can acquire a broader symbolic function in scientific knowledge production and communication.

**The Ideological Fetishism of Formal Verification** occurs when the bounded reliability of a formal check is made to function as an autonomous guarantee of broader scientific certainty, while the human, conceptual, semantic, and institutional relations that make the verification meaningful recede from view.

The paper does not argue that Lean is unreliable, that formal verification is merely ideological, or that all reliance on a proof assistant is irrational.

## 2. The three Žižekian operations

1. **Ideological fantasy — scene of closure.** A reproducible local check may be represented as the point at which wider scientific uncertainty is settled.
2. **Fetishism — objectal displacement.** The network that makes the verification meaningful may appear as an autonomous property of the machine.
3. **Disavowal — persistence despite acknowledged limits.** The broader symbolic investment may continue even when the relevant limits are known. This is a conditional hypothesis, not a required feature of every case.

The three operations are related but not a deterministic sequence. The Big Other and the absence of a final guarantor are structural background, not a fourth co-equal mechanism. Lean is not the Big Other.

## 3. The empirical architecture

For each of the four Lean cases, pair:
- the exact formal proposition and proof artifact;
- the actual kernel-level warrant;
- the semantic relation to the intended mathematical claim;
- the collective technical and human work;
- the institutional and public representation;
- counter-evidence and rival explanations.

The cases have different functions: Riemann is ambivalent; Navier–Stokes is the strongest candidate for scope enlargement and fetishistic displacement; Erdős/AlphaProof is a mixed case with visible human validation and provenance issues; Liquid Tensor is a strong counter-case emphasizing collective labour and semantic scrutiny.

## 4. Diagnostic threshold

A strong diagnosis requires:
- a real and bounded technical warrant;
- an identifiable enlargement beyond that warrant;
- a displacement of collective epistemic relations into apparent machine autonomy;
- reproduction of this representation in a repeated or institutionally consequential practice;
- exclusion of ordinary shorthand and context-preserving qualifications.

Disavowal requires the additional evidence of acknowledged limits and continued investment. No individual motive or unconscious belief is inferred from public wording alone.

## 5. Originality

The paper's contribution is not a new distinction between formal validity and semantic correspondence, a general theory of AI trust, or the first Žižekian critique of technology. It is a focused Žižekian account of the symbolic authority of Lean verification in contemporary mathematical and scientific knowledge production.
