# The Žižekian core argument: Lean and the social fantasy of scientific certainty

**Project:** *In Lean We Trust? The Ideology of Machine-Checked Proofs in Science*  
**Date:** 27 November 2026  
**Status:** core theoretical argument for development; not a completed manuscript or an empirical finding.

## 1. Thesis

This paper argues that Lean can acquire a social and ideological function beyond its technical role as a proof assistant. Its kernel provides a genuine, bounded form of formal assurance: it checks that a proof term establishes a specified proposition under a specified formal environment. Yet when this check circulates through scientific institutions and public communication, it can become a visible symbol of objectivity, rigor, and certainty. A Žižekian analysis asks why society invests this technical credential with such symbolic force, how it may conceal the collective labour and institutional mediation on which it depends, and how it helps organize the public relation to scientific uncertainty.

The claim is not that Lean is unreliable, that public trust is irrational, or that every use of verification is ideological. The argument concerns the social function of the verification credential and the conditions under which its meaning is enlarged beyond its formal warrant.

## 2. The philosophical problem: the social demand for a guarantee

Modern scientific knowledge is structurally inaccessible to most of those who rely on it. Its claims are produced through specialized instruments, mathematics, statistical methods, computational systems, and distributed research teams. Ordinary citizens cannot personally reproduce most experiments or inspect most proofs. This asymmetry is not a pathology; it is a normal condition of modern knowledge.

However, scientific claims have consequences far beyond the specialist communities that produce them. The public therefore encounters a persistent problem: how can one reasonably rely on knowledge whose production and validation one cannot personally survey?

Lean offers a distinctive answer to one part of this problem. It transforms a proof into a formal object that can be checked reproducibly by a small trusted kernel. The check is real, technical, and valuable. Its social appeal may derive partly from making a difficult-to-observe epistemic process appear as a visible and repeatable procedure.

The ideological question begins when this visible procedure is treated not merely as evidence for a specified formal proposition, but as a symbol of scientific certainty more generally.

## 3. Žižekian mechanism I: ideological fantasy of verification

### 3.1 Theoretical basis

In *The Sublime Object of Ideology* (1989), Žižek develops an account of ideology in which fantasy does not simply conceal an already complete social reality. Fantasy helps structure how subjects experience and sustain a social reality marked by antagonism, incompleteness, and inconsistency. The point is not that people consciously believe a false proposition; fantasy can organize the coordinates within which a practice appears coherent.

The paper should work from Žižek's treatment of fantasy and the social efficacy of ideology, especially the opening chapters of *The Sublime Object of Ideology*, rather than reduce the concept to wishful thinking or a desire for certainty.

### 3.2 Application

**Ideological fantasy of verification** is the hypothesis that machine checking can be staged as the place where the uncertainty of scientific knowledge is finally closed. The formal checker becomes the visible scene in which a complex, distributed, and difficult-to-survey process appears to culminate in a simple verdict: verified.

This fantasy does not require the claim that Lean literally verifies empirical reality. It can operate through the public association of formal verification with objectivity, mathematical rigor, and finality. The kernel's real reliability supplies the material support for the broader symbolic expectation.

### 3.3 What must be demonstrated

Look for repeated public formulations that move from “this formal proof term is accepted for this proposition” to “the scientific problem is solved,” “the result is certain,” or “the machine has settled the question.” Compare these formulations with the primary theorem, assumptions, semantic bridge, expert review, and status of acceptance.

A single dramatic headline is insufficient. The analysis requires patterns across institutional announcements, media coverage, and public uptake, alongside counterexamples that preserve the exact scope.

## 4. Žižekian mechanism II: epistemic fetishism

### 4.1 Theoretical basis

Žižek's account of fetishism draws on Marx's analysis of commodity fetishism and Lacanian disavowal. A social relation or product of collective activity can appear as if its power were an intrinsic property of a thing. The issue is not simply that an object is admired; it is that the social relations that produce its apparent power are displaced or rendered invisible.

The phrase **epistemic fetishism of Lean verification** is this paper's proposed analytic extension. It is not presented as a term coined by Žižek.

### 4.2 Application

Lean's checking power is real, but the relevance of a Lean result depends on many relations:
- researchers choose the problem and formulate the intended theorem;
- mathematicians and formalizers select definitions and encode the statement;
- developers maintain Lean, libraries, compilers, and infrastructure;
- experts determine whether the formal statement matches the intended mathematics;
- institutions and journals assess novelty, significance, and responsibility;
- communicators explain what has and has not been established.

Epistemic fetishism becomes plausible when the reliability produced by this network appears as an autonomous property of “the machine,” while the network itself recedes from view.

### 4.3 What must be demonstrated

Identify the collective labour and institutional dependencies in the primary record. Then examine whether public representations foreground Lean as the sole or decisive source of authority, omit necessary semantic or human contributions, or present the machine's output as self-authenticating. Compare with texts that explicitly credit the human and institutional network.

Do not infer fetishism from the mere statement that Lean verified a theorem. That statement can be exactly correct. The relevant question is whether the social representation makes the machine appear to possess authority independently of the relations that make its verification meaningful.

## 5. Žižekian mechanism III: the Big Other and the guarantee that does not exist

### 5.1 Conceptual precision

The Lacanian Big Other is not a super-person, a software system, or a real institution that possesses all knowledge. It names the symbolic locus of language, law, norms, and the supposed consistency of the social order. Žižek repeatedly stresses that the Big Other is barred or lacking: there is no ultimate, complete guarantor that can guarantee the symbolic order from outside.

Therefore, the paper must not say “Lean is the Big Other.” The more precise hypothesis is that Lean can be **positioned as a technical credential within a symbolic order that seeks a guarantor of scientific knowledge**.

The subject-supposed-to-know is related but distinct. It concerns the attribution of knowledge within transference. It may help analyze how a knowing position is attributed to experts or institutions, but it should not be used as a loose synonym for public trust in software.

### 5.2 Application

Scientific institutions, experts, journals, and technical systems can be treated as if they jointly occupy a place from which a claim is guaranteed. Lean may become one particularly visible credential in that arrangement because its output is presented as mechanically checkable and reproducible.

The ideological tension is that the public may seek a final guarantee from a system whose actual scope is necessarily limited. Lean can certify a formal derivation; it cannot itself guarantee that the formal statement captures the intended scientific problem, that the assumptions are empirically appropriate, that the result is important, or that every institutional judgment is correct.

### 5.3 The productive paradox

The Big Other does not exist as a complete guarantor, yet social practices can continue to act as if such a guarantee were available. The paper's hypothesis is that machine verification may serve as one contemporary support for this “as if”: a real technical check can be invested with the symbolic function of a final guarantee, even though no technical system can close every level of scientific uncertainty.

This is the deepest Lacanian point in the paper. The machine is not the final authority; it can function as a sign through which the social order stages the existence of such an authority.

## 6. The central theoretical proposition: verification relocates uncertainty

Lean can sharply reduce uncertainty about whether a proof term follows from a formal proposition under a specified environment. It does not eliminate uncertainty about:
1. whether the formal proposition captures the intended mathematical or scientific question;
2. whether the assumptions and definitions are appropriate;
3. whether the result is novel and significant;
4. whether the argument is understood and integrated into the discipline;
5. whether institutions and communicators represent the result accurately;
6. how non-specialists should reasonably rely on the claim.

Thus:

**Verification does not abolish uncertainty; it relocates uncertainty.**

The technical success of the kernel may make semantic, interpretive, institutional, and communicative judgments more visible and more consequential. The social fantasy arises if the certainty achieved at one level is made to stand for certainty at all levels.

## 7. What Žižek adds beyond social epistemology

Hardwig and social epistemology explain why epistemic dependence is unavoidable and can be rational. A non-specialist may be justified in relying on experts, testimony, and institutions without personally reproducing every proof. This is indispensable for distinguishing legitimate trust from credulity.

Žižek adds a different explanatory level:
- Hardwig asks under what conditions reliance is epistemically warranted.
- Žižek asks what symbolic and ideological function a particular form of warrant performs in a social order.
- Hardwig explains the division of epistemic labour.
- Žižek helps explain how the product of that labour can appear as an autonomous object of certainty.
- Hardwig analyzes justified dependence.
- Žižek analyzes the fantasy of a complete guarantor and the practices that sustain its appearance.

The paper's originality is not the distinction between formal correctness and semantic correspondence, nor the claim that scientific knowledge is socially distributed. Its proposed contribution is the Žižekian explanation of why a bounded formal-verification credential can acquire the symbolic function of a guarantee of scientific certainty in society at large.

## 8. Four Lean cases as theoretical tests

### Riemann hypothesis

Strong machine verification coexists with incomplete human mathematical exposition. Test whether public narratives treat checker acceptance as equivalent to mathematical understanding or final social acceptance. Also examine counter-narratives that clearly separate them.

### Navier–Stokes

The Lean formalization and natural-language paper have been reported to differ in specific estimates. This is the strongest test of whether kernel validity is symbolically allowed to stand in for source-text correspondence. The mismatch is not a kernel failure or a refutation of the theorem.

### Erdős / AlphaProof Nexus

Lean proof search is embedded in a disclosed human-AI workflow, with human validation of formal statements. This tests whether visible mediation resists the machine-autonomy narrative and supplies a counterexample to automatic fetishism.

### Liquid Tensor Experiment

Scholze's account emphasizes human mathematical interaction and scrutiny alongside Lean checking. The final project record credits a large collaborative network. This historical case tests whether trust can be generated through collective practice rather than transferred to the machine as an autonomous authority.

## 9. What would count as evidence for each mechanism?

| Mechanism | Evidence required | What is not enough |
|---|---|---|
| Ideological fantasy | Recurrent representations of verification as closure, finality, or a guarantee beyond the formal scope | A single headline saying “proved” |
| Epistemic fetishism | Traceable collective labour plus public displacement of that labour in favour of autonomous machine authority | Merely naming Lean as the checker |
| Big Other / symbolic guarantee | Evidence that Lean is positioned within a wider symbolic order as a credential that appears to settle otherwise unmanageable uncertainty | Calling Lean “the Big Other” |
| Cynical disavowal | Acknowledged limits plus continued institutional practice that relies on an enlarged guarantee | A caveat appearing beside a strong claim |

## 10. Rival explanations and safeguards

1. **Legitimate epistemic dependence:** the public may reasonably rely on the checker and qualified experts.
2. **Ordinary compression:** media may simplify while preserving the correct scope in the body text.
3. **Institutional reputation:** trust may be directed at universities or mathematicians rather than Lean.
4. **Transparency:** detailed disclosure of human work may actively resist fetishization.
5. **Public scepticism:** AI involvement may reduce trust rather than increase it.
6. **Technical warrant:** a fully checked formal theorem genuinely deserves confidence at the formal level.

The analysis must retain counterexamples and cannot diagnose unconscious motives from public texts. Cynical ideology should be used only if evidence establishes acknowledged limitations and their practical disavowal.

## 11. Provisional conclusion

The strongest version of the paper's thesis is:

**In contemporary science, Lean can become more than a proof assistant: it can function as a symbolic credential through which society imagines that the uncertainty of specialized knowledge has been rendered controllable. A Žižekian critique shows how this credential may become fetishized when the collective labour and semantic mediation that sustain it disappear from view, and how the machine can be positioned as if it guaranteed a scientific order that has no final guarantor.**

This is a theoretical hypothesis to be tested against the four Lean cases, not a presupposed verdict on the technology or its users.
