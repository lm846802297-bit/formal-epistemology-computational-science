# Coding guide (pilot)

Each coded record must preserve the exact public wording, the source artifact, the locator, date/version, the relevant theorem/definition/hypotheses, and the reason for the classification.

## Primary scope code

- **Accurate:** the statement's explicit claim is supported by the cited formal artifact and does not imply a broader target.
- **Reasonable compression:** details are omitted, but surrounding context preserves the intended scope or points to a sufficient qualification.
- **Ambiguous:** the wording supports materially different readings, or the relevant artifact/qualification cannot be identified. Record rival readings; do not force a verdict.
- **Substantive scope expansion:** the statement attributes a stronger conclusion to the formal check than the cited evidence establishes. Identify the exact missing inferential link and check whether another linked document supplies it.

## Secondary target code

Record whether the statement concerns:
1. local deduction in a formal system;
2. translation or interoperability between formal records;
3. correspondence between formal definitions and a source mathematical/physical formalism;
4. instantiation of theorem hypotheses by a model or protocol;
5. a claim about an empirical or physical target;
6. an institutional or public verdict that a scientific question is settled.

## Rival explanations

Record whether the wording may be explained by ordinary shorthand, genre conventions, reliance on expert testimony, an explicit qualification elsewhere, or an ambiguous referent.

## Inference limits

Cues such as “verified,” “proved,” “formal,” “complete,” or “settled” are textual observations, not evidence of a reader's mental state. Public texts can support claims about representation and attribution of authority; claims about actual belief require reader evidence.
