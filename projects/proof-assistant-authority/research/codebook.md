# Coding guide for the Žižekian study of Lean verification

Every record preserves the exact public wording or institutional practice, source and locator, date/version, formal theorem and proof artifact, environment/axioms/dependencies, checking status, intended claim, specification relation, and counter-evidence.

## 1. Technical baseline

Record separately:
- intended mathematical claim P;
- formal proposition P*;
- proof object and provenance;
- checking procedure and trusted base;
- axioms, definitions, libraries, and dependencies;
- independent replication/checking routes;
- semantic/specification audit;
- institutional review and recognition.

Kernel acceptance establishes derivational validity relative to the specified formal environment. It does not automatically establish the specification relation P ↔ P*.

## 2. Scope relation (S)

Assess two distinct forms of scope extension; do not conflate them.

- **Warrant-scope extension (S_w):** a public/institutional claim attributes to the credential a warrant broader than the formal proposition and its documented specification relation.
- **Agency-attribution extension (S_a):** a public/institutional claim extends the machine's agency from a bounded operation (for example, autonomous proof search and proof-term generation) to the broader achievement (for example, autonomously resolving a mathematical problem).

For either form, record the exact linguistic or inferential shift and the relevant technical baseline.

- **Absent:** the public claim stays within the checked proposition/specification relation (S_w) or accurately attributes only the bounded machine operation (S_a).
- **Ambiguous:** the object of the claim or the scope of the attributed warrant/agency is unclear.
- **Possible extension:** wording permits a broader reading, but context or qualification prevents a firm diagnosis.
- **Supported extension:** the source attributes broader warrant (S_w) or broader agency (S_a) than the bounded operation itself supplies.

An S_a finding does not imply that the mathematical result is false, that the formal proof is defective, or that expert validation failed. Record whether the claim concerns proof validity, statement correspondence, attribution of agency, or more than one of these.


## 3. Objectal displacement (O)

- **Absent:** the credential is presented as one component of a visible technical, semantic, and institutional network.
- **Weak/possible:** mediation is compressed or backgrounded, but autonomous authority is not clearly attributed to the credential.
- **Moderate:** the credential is repeatedly foregrounded as the source of broader authority while necessary relations are backgrounded.
- **Strong candidate:** the credential is represented or used as a self-sufficient bearer of broader authority, and the displacement is supported by matched source evidence.

Omission of contributors alone does not establish objectal displacement.

## 4. Social efficacy (E)

Record whether the representation has a documented role in:
- institutional endorsement or certification;
- acceptance or closure of a dispute;
- allocation of credibility or expert authority;
- publication or review decisions;
- funding, deployment, or other institutional action;
- repeated routine reliance.

Code as **none established**, **possible**, or **documented**. A public announcement alone does not establish that the Lean credential caused the resulting recognition or decision.

## 5. Five-moment formation and reproduction mechanism

For each case, record evidence for each moment separately:

1. **Real technical reliability:** kernel acceptance, reproducibility, independent checking, and the exact formal proposition/environment.
2. **Distributed epistemic labour:** theorem formulation, formalization, proof construction, library/toolchain maintenance, semantic comparison, expert review, and institutional assessment.
3. **Credential condensation:** the use of “Lean-verified” or equivalent language as a portable summary in papers, abstracts, institutional announcements, repositories, or media.
4. **Objectal displacement and symbolic authority:** evidence that authority generated by the network is attributed to the credential as if it were autonomous, and that the credential is made to signify more than the local check. Assess S, O, and E separately.
5. **Fetishistic disavowal and reproduction:** evidence that the same public communication chain explicitly acknowledges human/semantic mediation and nevertheless continues to present the machine as the autonomous bearer of the whole achievement. The wider reading must remain available in context, and the representation must have a public-authority function. The scope shift may concern agency attribution (S_a), even when the mathematical result itself is properly warranted. No inference about private motives is required.

The first two moments are enabling conditions; the third is communicative condensation; the fourth is the fetishistic conversion; the fifth is a conditional feedback mechanism. The sequence is analytical, not a deterministic causal law. The fifth moment is not inferred merely from knowledge of Lean’s technical scope plus continued rational reliance on it.

## 6. Fantasy of closure

- **Absent:** no representation of the local check as closure of wider uncertainty.
- **Weak/possible:** isolated closure language with no documented practical role.
- **Moderate:** repeated representations link formal checking to broader certainty, but qualifications and mediation remain visible.
- **Strong candidate:** a bounded formal verdict is repeatedly or consequentially staged as the settlement of uncertainty beyond its formal scope.

Fantasy is not synonymous with exaggeration. Identify the gap or antagonism whose appearance of closure is being organized.

## 7. Fetishistic disavowal: evidence categories

- **None established:** no traceable co-presence of acknowledged mediation and a continued wider autonomy/authority representation.
- **Co-presence only:** a source acknowledges technical or semantic mediation while also using autonomy or verification language, but the latter is clearly limited to the actual scope of the process.
- **Possible:** the same communication chain acknowledges mediation and continues a broader symbolic representation, but the wider reading or practical role remains ambiguous.
- **Supported at the level of public representation:** the same actor or institutional communication chain explicitly identifies human/semantic mediation and nevertheless retains an autonomous-machine framing that can reasonably be read as attributing the whole achievement to the machine. The framing has a recognizable public-authority function, such as a research abstract, institutional headline, or official account of the achievement.
- **Cynical disavowal candidate:** evidence suggests strategic concealment or manipulation of the acknowledged gap between knowledge and public/practical action.

Distinguish three cases:
- **Rational reliance:** the actor knows what Lean checks and relies on it for that exact task, or combines it with independent semantic review that establishes the intended claim. This is not disavowal.
- **Fetishistic disavowal:** the same public communication chain acknowledges the human or semantic conditions of the result while continuing to present the machine as the apparent autonomous bearer of the whole achievement. This is a structural claim about public attribution and its authority function, not an inference about private mental states.
- **Cynical disavowal:** the actor strategically obscures the acknowledged gap between knowledge and public/practical action.

Do not infer unconscious motives from wording alone. Public texts establish representations and documented practices, not private mental states.

## 8. Three empirical planes

- **Discursive:** explicit statements in papers, announcements, institutional pages, and media.
- **Material-institutional:** repositories, checking workflows, expert review, publication, and institutional certification.
- **Spontaneous-practical:** repeated routine reliance on the credential as a broader warrant. Code only when repeated practices are documented.

## 9. Rival explanations

Record whether the apparent pattern is better explained by:
- accurate shorthand or genre conventions;
- explicit qualifications in the same communication chain;
- justified reliance on experts and a trusted kernel;
- visible semantic audit and human validation;
- institutional reputation;
- later correction or clarification;
- insufficient evidence.

## 10. Inference limits

- Public texts establish representations, not audience belief.
- A technical mismatch is not automatically a kernel failure or a refutation of the theorem.
- A preprint critique is not equivalent to peer-reviewed mathematical adjudication.
- Source-level inspection is not a repository rebuild.
- Do not infer ideological fetishism unless S, O, and E are separately supported.
