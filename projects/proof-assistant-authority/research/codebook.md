# Coding guide

Every record must preserve the exact public wording, source artifact, locator, date/version, relevant theorem/definition/hypotheses, review status, and reason for the classification.

## 1. Claim-scope code
- **Accurate:** explicit claim is supported by the cited formal artifact and does not imply a broader target.
- **Reasonable compression:** details are omitted, but surrounding context preserves intended scope or points to a sufficient qualification.
- **Ambiguous:** materially different readings remain possible or relevant evidence is missing.
- **Substantive scope expansion:** claim attributes a stronger conclusion to the formal check than the artifact and documented semantic bridge establish. Identify the exact missing inferential link.

## 2. AI contribution code
Record whether AI:
1. proposed a conjecture;
2. found a proof strategy or key lemma;
3. generated a natural-language proof;
4. generated a formal proof;
5. translated a natural-language proof into a formal language;
6. searched or verified an existing proof;
7. worked in a human-AI collaborative workflow.

Do not infer “AI solved it” from an AI-use statement without identifying what the system actually did and what humans did.

## 3. Verification and recognition dimensions
Code separately:
- formal statement: exact theorem, assumptions, definitions, and target;
- formal proof: compiles/checks, allowed axioms, placeholders, independent checker status;
- semantic correspondence: whether the formal statement and proof match the human-readable source;
- mathematical understanding: whether experts can explain/reconstruct the argument;
- expert review: self-review, author-team review, independent review, peer review;
- institutional recognition: university, journal, learned society, prize body;
- public status: reported claim, accepted result, unresolved dispute, retraction/correction.

Do not collapse these dimensions into one “verified” label.

## 4. Public framing and reception
For each media or public-discussion text, code:
- authority source emphasized: AI system, proof assistant, named expert, research team, university, company, journal, scientific community;
- certainty frame: claim, breakthrough, proof, solved, certified, accepted, still under review;
- human-labour visibility: foregrounded, backgrounded, omitted, or explicitly acknowledged;
- public interpretive frame: awe/excitement, trust, scepticism, anxiety/job displacement, institutional distrust, confusion about formal checking, distinction between checking and understanding, or other;
- audience/source type: representative survey, expert interview, institutional announcement, professional media, general media, self-selected online discussion.

## 5. Rival explanations
Record ordinary shorthand, genre conventions, reliance on expert testimony, explicit qualifications elsewhere, institutional reputation, audience literacy, and ambiguity. Strong wording is not automatically distortion.

## 6. Inference limits
- Public texts establish representations and invitations to trust, not actual belief.
- Self-selected online posts establish that an interpretation exists, not its prevalence.
- General trust surveys establish background attitudes, not causal effects of a specific proof announcement.
- No unconscious motive, transference, or ideological attachment may be attributed to an individual from wording alone.
- Ideological analysis is a structural interpretation that must be supported by repeated relations among claims, practices, and institutional arrangements, not by isolated rhetoric.
