# Public-text pilot protocol

## Unit of analysis
One checkable proposition about verification in a public text, paired with the exact theorem, definitions, and recorded assumptions that could support it. Capture dates and version links. Analyze claims rather than assigning motives to people.

## Initial comparative design
Use three distinct data layers: a primary formal artifact, its project-authored description, and an independent public retelling. Begin with OS as a pilot benchmark, then select at least two separate proof-assistant projects that have documents at all three layers. Fix the repositories, date window, inclusion rule, and unit of coding before collecting confirmatory evidence. Retain contrary or scope-preserving examples; report omissions and ambiguous cases.

## Evidence and rivals
Compare the claim with its linked artifacts, including which object, assumptions, and verification relation are asserted. Check whether scope is stated in surrounding text. Consider concise-but-accurate shorthand, expert testimony with supporting proof elsewhere, genre conventions, ambiguity, and genuine scope expansion. A textual pattern can establish how a claim was presented, not why readers believed it. Interviews or a controlled presentation study would be a separate phase requiring an appropriate ethics and consent review.

## Stopping rule
If the pilot finds only ordinary shorthand or cannot reliably distinguish scope preservation from expansion, narrow the thesis or stop. If expansion is observed, report the evidence and rival explanations before proposing a mechanism.

## Starting references
- John Hardwig, [Epistemic Dependence](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2026523).
- Slavoj Žižek, [The Interpassive Subject](https://www.lacan.com/zizek-pompidou.htm). A potential interpretive comparison, not an empirical result.
