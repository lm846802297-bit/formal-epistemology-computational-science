# Public-text and social-reception study protocol

## Research question
How does machine verification become a publicly legible symbol of the reliability of scientific knowledge, and what ideological functions does this symbol perform in contemporary society?

## Unit of analysis
A claim about a scientific or mathematical result in a dated public text, paired with the primary formal artifact and the exact theorem, definitions, assumptions, and review status that could support it. Preserve URLs, version/commit identifiers, publication dates, and locators.

## Four-case design
Study four cases that differ in AI contribution, formal verification, human review, and institutional recognition:
1. Riemann hypothesis — Claude-assisted discovery and Lean certification.
2. Waring's problem — GPT-6.2 Astra credited with carrying out a preprint later rewritten by the authors; AI self-refereeing.
3. Navier–Stokes — OpenAI multi-agent discovery, Lean formalization, and an independent critique of semantic correspondence.
4. Erdős problems — AlphaProof Nexus proof search, Lean verification, and human statement validation.

For each case, collect:
- the primary theorem and formal artifact;
- the AI-use statement and the role of human authors;
- the formal verification method and pinned source version;
- independent checking, peer review, and institutional status;
- project/institutional announcement;
- independent media coverage;
- public discussion where available.

## Society-wide evidence
Use population surveys to contextualize public trust in science, scientists, and AI. Do not treat general trust-in-science surveys as direct measurements of trust in machine-checked proofs. Use public online discussions as qualitative examples of interpretive repertoires only; they are self-selected and not representative. Keep the public's invitation to trust (textual evidence) distinct from actual public belief (survey/interview/experimental evidence).

## Coding and rival explanations
Compare the public claim with its linked artifacts and context. Consider ordinary shorthand, genre conventions, legitimate dependence on expert testimony, institutional reputation, explicit qualifications elsewhere, ambiguity, and genuine scope expansion. Keep formal correctness, semantic correspondence, mathematical understanding, peer review, institutional recognition, and public trust as separate dimensions.

## Theory use
Use Hardwig to explain legitimate epistemic dependence; Žižek's ideological fantasy, fetishism, and cynical ideology as hypotheses about how authority and certainty are represented; Lacan's subject-supposed-to-know as a supporting concept for attribution of a knowing position. Do not infer unconscious motive, transference, or belief from public text alone. Do not label every trust relationship ideological.

## Stopping rule
The initial phase is complete when all four cases have pinned primary artifacts and dated public accounts, at least three cases have an independent public retelling, the public-trust baseline is documented, and each case is coded for AI contribution, verification, review, institutional status, public framing, and evidence limits. If the evidence does not support ideological scope expansion, report legitimate trust and scope-preserving cases rather than forcing a critical verdict.

## Out of scope for this phase
No new public survey, interview, or experiment. Such work is a later option only if the text-and-existing-survey analysis identifies a specific unresolved question about actual audience belief.
