# Revised controlling theoretical framework

**Project:** *In Lean We Trust?*  
**Date:** 27 November 2026  
**Status:** controlling conceptual summary after anonymous referee revision. For the complete model and diagnostic rules, see THEORETICAL_MODEL.md. For the complete paper, see manuscript/IN_LEAN_WE_TRUST_REVISED_DRAFT.md.

## 1. Object

The object is the public and institutional credential “Lean-verified,” not Lean software in general, not all formal proof, and not generic trust in AI.

## 2. Central research question

Under what conditions does the genuine but bounded warrant of Lean verification acquire an ideological function as an apparently self-sufficient guarantee of broader mathematical authority?

## 3. Central hypothesis

The ideological fetishism of formal verification is the objectal displacement through which the socially and semantically mediated authority of a machine-checked result appears to reside autonomously in the credential “Lean-verified,” allowing the warrant of a bounded formal check to stand for a wider mathematical claim.

This is a proposed Žižekian extension and a conditional diagnosis, not a claim that every use of Lean is ideological.

## 4. Technical baseline

Adopt the component-based warrant architecture developed by Vallverdú (2026) and the distinctions among derivational validity, representational fidelity, and epistemic significance developed by Kallel and El Louadi (2026). Do not claim these distinctions as original.

Lean’s kernel checks a proof term for a specified formal proposition under a specified environment. The relation between that proposition and the intended mathematical claim is a separate specification relation. It can be rigorously audited; it is not automatically established by kernel acceptance.

## 5. Žižekian mechanisms

1. **Ideological fantasy:** the local check may stage a scene in which a wider uncertainty appears to have been closed.
2. **Fetishism:** authority generated by technical, semantic, and institutional relations may appear to reside autonomously in the credential.
3. **Disavowal:** acknowledged limits may coexist with continued investment in a broader guarantee. This is secondary and requires higher evidence.

Fantasy and fetishism are related but not mechanically sequential. Disavowal is not required for the core diagnosis. The Big Other is background, not a fourth mechanism.

## 6. Diagnostic requirements

The technical warrant is the baseline, not a positive ideology indicator. A strong diagnosis requires:

- **S — Scope extension:** the credential is made to warrant more than the checked proposition and documented specification relation establish.
- **O — Objectal displacement:** the credential appears to contain autonomous authority while the relations that make it meaningful are displaced.
- **E — Social efficacy:** this representation has a documented role in legitimation, recognition, credibility allocation, institutional action, or repeated practice.

A strong diagnosis requires S ∧ O ∧ E. Omission of human contributors or a single exaggerated phrase is insufficient.

## 7. Empirical scope

Four purposive maximum-variation cases:
- Riemann hypothesis: formal certification, institutional authorization, AI-mediated exposition, and foundational challenge.
- Navier–Stokes: formal declarations and the natural-language proof.
- Erdős / AlphaProof Nexus: human validation, benchmark scope, and artifact provenance.
- Liquid Tensor: explicit semantic sanity checks and visible collective labour as a counter-case.

No case is presumed to meet the full diagnostic threshold. Current assessments are in THEORY_CASE_TESTS.md.

## 8. Literature and originality

The paper does not claim to be the first Žižekian critique of technology, the first ideology critique of AI, the first social epistemology of proof assistants, or the first component-based analysis of mathematical warrant. The contribution is narrower: a Žižekian diagnostic account of the symbolic and legitimating function of the credential “Lean-verified.”
