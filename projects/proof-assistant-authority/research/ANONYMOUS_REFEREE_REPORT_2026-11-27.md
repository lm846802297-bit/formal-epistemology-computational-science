# Anonymous Referee Report

**Manuscript:** *The Ideological Fetishism of Formal Verification: A Žižekian Critique of Scientific Knowledge Production*  
**Manuscript file reviewed:** `manuscript/IN_LEAN_WE_TRUST_FULL_DRAFT.md`  
**Review date:** 27 November 2026  
**Recommendation:** **Major revision; not ready for submission**  
**Review scope:** theoretical originality, fidelity to Žižek's primary texts, inferential validity, case-study design, literature positioning, and manuscript-level scholarly apparatus.

---

## 1. Overall assessment

The manuscript addresses a genuinely interesting question: how can a technically valid, bounded formal verification acquire a symbolic and institutional authority that exceeds the proposition checked by the proof assistant? Its strongest intuition is that the reliability of Lean need not be an illusion for the authority attributed to it to acquire an ideological function. This is a promising starting point for a contribution at the intersection of philosophy of science, philosophy of mathematical practice, social epistemology, and critical theory.

The paper also has several real strengths:

1. It does not equate ideology with technical error or false belief.
2. It distinguishes kernel-level derivational validity from statement identity, semantic correspondence, mathematical understanding, and institutional recognition.
3. It takes the epistemic value of proof checking seriously, particularly in light of Duede and Davey (2025).
4. It recognizes the Marxian structure of fetishism as a relation appearing in object-form, rather than merely as excessive admiration of a machine.
5. It retains counter-evidence and does not accuse named researchers of cynical intent.
6. It includes Liquid Tensor as a potential counter-case rather than treating every Lean project as ideological by definition.

These strengths do not yet add up to a demonstrated philosophical contribution. In its present form, the manuscript is best described as a promising conceptual essay with illustrative case material, not yet as a fully supported comparative study. The central concept is plausible, but its distinctiveness, derivation, and empirical applicability remain under-argued.

**Recommendation: major revision.** The paper should not be rejected as an unpromising project. It should not be submitted in its current form.

## 2. Major issue I: the Žižekian derivation is not yet sufficiently precise

### 2.1 The manuscript moves too quickly from Žižek to its own concept

The manuscript correctly draws on Žižek's rejection of a simple false-consciousness model. It is also right that an accurate statement can participate in an ideological relation. However, the paper sometimes moves too quickly from this general premise to the claim that a bounded Lean credential can perform a broader ideological function.

The fact that truth and ideology are not mutually exclusive does not itself establish that a true technical statement is ideological. The paper must specify the mediating relation: which social antagonism, inconsistency, lack, or institutional practice is being organized by the credential? What exactly does the verification credential make possible, sustain, or conceal? What would be different in the relevant practice if that credential were absent?

Without this intermediate account, the argument risks becoming:

- Žižek says ideology is not reducible to falsehood;
- Lean is sometimes described in compressed terms;
- therefore Lean's authority may be ideological.

That inference is invalid without a more specific account of ideological efficacy.

**Required revision:** distinguish (a) factual accuracy, (b) rhetorical overstatement, (c) institutional legitimation, and (d) Žižekian ideological function. These are not synonyms. The paper must show why the last category explains something the first three do not.

### 2.2 The fantasy argument risks reducing fantasy to overgeneralization

The manuscript defines the fantasy as a scene in which the kernel's local certainty comes to symbolize the closure of wider scientific uncertainty. This is a plausible application, but it currently resembles a general claim about overconfidence or rhetorical scope inflation.

In Žižek, fantasy is not simply a false picture, a reassuring metaphor, or an exaggerated claim. It structures the coordinates through which a social reality is experienced and can sustain the consistency of an order despite its constitutive lack. The paper needs to identify that constitutive lack in the relevant scientific practice.

Possible candidate: the unavoidable gap between a formal object and the intended mathematical claim cannot be closed by another layer of formal checking alone. However, the manuscript must show why the verification credential is invested with the function of covering or managing this gap, rather than merely serving as one well-understood component in an ordinary warrant architecture.

**Required revision:** formulate the specific lack/antagonism that the fantasy organizes, then show how the “verified” scene stages its temporary closure. Do not infer fantasy merely from the presence of a compact technical verdict.

### 2.3 The fetishism argument is promising but needs a stricter Marxian distinction

The manuscript appropriately states that Lean is not a commodity in Marx's technical economic sense. Nevertheless, it sometimes treats the movement from collective epistemic labour to machine autonomy as if it were already equivalent to commodity fetishism.

Marx's analysis is historically specific: it concerns the social form of commodity production and the appearance of social relations among producers as relations among things. A structural analogy is possible, but it must be argued rather than assumed.

There is also a conceptual problem in the manuscript's description of collective labour. The human and technical network does not produce mathematical truth in the same way that labour produces commodities. It produces, maintains, interprets, checks, and circulates formal artifacts and the warrant for connecting them to intended claims. The manuscript now acknowledges this distinction, but the theoretical argument should make it central rather than leave it as a caveat.

**Required revision:** define the object of fetishization precisely. Is it:
- the proof assistant as an apparently autonomous epistemic agent;
- the Lean-verified artifact as an apparently self-authenticating object;
- the institutional credential “Lean-verified” as a condensed warrant;
- or the whole technical-institutional verification apparatus?

The current manuscript moves between all four. Choose one primary object and explain the relations condensed into it.

### 2.4 Disavowal is conceptually overextended

The manuscript draws on Žižek's discussion of cynical ideology and Black (2025) on cynical and fetishistic disavowal. This is useful, but the two forms are not yet applied with enough precision.

An actor's acknowledgement that Lean has limits, followed by continued reliance on Lean, is not automatically disavowal. It may simply be rational reliance on a trusted checker within its proper scope. Even when a public description is too broad, the cause may be genre compression, institutional shorthand, or a later correction.

The paper correctly states that it has not established cynical disavowal in any case. It should go further: **disavowal should be treated as a secondary, optional diagnostic, not as one of the three equally established mechanisms of the central concept.** The core paper can stand on fantasy and fetishism. Disavowal can be retained as a higher-evidence extension.

**Required revision:** make explicit that the two-form distinction is developed in later scholarship and is not simply a taxonomy that can be read off Žižek's own text. Do not infer a subject's unconscious relation from public texts alone.

## 3. Major issue II: the T–E–F–R criterion is not yet a valid operational test

The manuscript proposes:

\[
T\land E\land F\land R
\]

where T is technical warrant, E scope enlargement, F fetishistic displacement, and R reproduction.

This is a useful attempt to prevent arbitrary diagnoses, but it is not yet methodologically stable.

### 3.1 T is a background condition, not a diagnostic indicator

If the research object is the ideological use of formal verification, a genuine technical credential is indeed a condition of possibility. But T does not discriminate between ideological and non-ideological cases: every case involving Lean can satisfy it. It belongs in the case description, not necessarily in the diagnostic conjunction.

### 3.2 E and F are not independent as currently defined

The manuscript defines E as enlargement beyond the technical warrant and F as the appearance of autonomous machine authority. In practice, the same piece of rhetoric may be used to establish both. This risks double-counting one observation.

The paper needs distinct evidence:
- E: a precise comparison between the formal proposition and the broader claim attributed to it.
- F: evidence that the social/semantic/institutional relations are represented or practically treated as intrinsic to the machine, not merely omitted from a short description.

A scope mismatch does not automatically demonstrate fetishistic displacement. Conversely, a machine may be represented as autonomous even when its formal proposition is accurately described.

### 3.3 R is asserted but not demonstrated in the four cases

R requires recurrence or institutional/practical consequence. The manuscript's cases mostly identify announcements, repositories, media descriptions, and subsequent critiques. It does not yet document a repeated routine in which the Lean credential is used as a default warrant for broader claims.

The paper repeatedly acknowledges this limitation, but still presents the model as if the four cases have tested its operation. They have not yet established R.

**Required revision:** either (a) collect repeated evidence for R within a clearly bounded institutional communication chain, or (b) remove R from the necessary threshold and distinguish a case-level instance of fetishistic representation from a reproduced institutional practice. The manuscript must not claim both that R is required and that the current cases establish the full diagnosis when they do not.

### 3.4 Suggested revised diagnostic architecture

Separate four elements:

1. **Technical baseline:** exact proposition, proof term, axioms, environment, checker.
2. **Scope relation:** exact difference between the formal target and the broader claim.
3. **Objectal displacement:** evidence that the credential or checker appears to contain an autonomous authority, with the mediating relations backgrounded or displaced.
4. **Social efficacy:** evidence that this representation organizes action, recognition, allocation of credibility, or institutional legitimation.

The fourth item is what makes the analysis specifically Žižekian rather than a simple discourse critique. Where social efficacy is not documented, the paper should make a more modest claim about a representational pattern or a possible ideological function, not a fully established ideological practice.

## 4. Major issue III: the four cases do not yet support the same comparative inference

### 4.1 The cases are heterogeneous

The Riemann and Erdős cases concern AI-assisted mathematical discovery followed by Lean formalization. Navier–Stokes concerns a specific claimed analytical result and a disputed correspondence between a natural-language proof and Lean declarations. Liquid Tensor is a human-led formalization project, not an AI-generated mathematical discovery.

This heterogeneity can be analytically productive, but the manuscript does not yet explain the comparative logic. It sometimes treats all four as examples of the same social process, while their production histories, audiences, institutions, and public claims differ substantially.

**Required revision:** define a common unit of analysis. A suitable unit may be “a public or institutional claim that invokes Lean verification as a warrant,” paired with its exact formal artifact and semantic bridge. Then explain why Liquid Tensor is a counter-case within this unit, despite its different historical and production context.

### 4.2 Riemann: the evidence currently points more strongly to layered certification than fetishism

The Riemann case includes a joint Lean FRO/Mathlib statement, independent checking, statement comparison, a compact formalization, and a substantial AI-assisted exposition. These developments make the technical and institutional mediation unusually visible.

The paper mentions compressed “AI solves RH” narratives, but does not systematically identify the relevant sources, compare their wording with the primary technical records, or show how those representations affected recognition or practice.

**Current judgement:** a useful case for distinguishing machine certification, mathematical exposition, expert understanding, and institutional recognition. It is not presently strong evidence of fetishism.

### 4.3 Navier–Stokes: strongest case, but still short of the full thesis

The m+4/m+5 discrepancy and pressure-flux difference provide a concrete semantic-correspondence issue. The manuscript is correct not to call this a kernel failure or a refutation of the theorem.

However, the existence of a mismatch between a formal artifact and a natural-language paper does not by itself prove that Lean was fetishized. The paper needs a precise analysis of:
- the original announcement's exact wording;
- the exact Lean theorem declarations and pinned repository state;
- the natural-language claims those declarations are said to represent;
- any qualifications in the announcement;
- subsequent responses and whether the credential continued to be invoked in a broader sense after the mismatch was public.

**Current judgement:** strongest candidate for scope enlargement; fetishistic displacement and social reproduction remain to be demonstrated.

### 4.4 Erdős / AlphaProof: provenance audit is not itself evidence of ideological authority

The #26 and #741 file/prose discrepancies are valuable. They demonstrate why statement identity and artifact provenance matter. But a technical provenance mismatch is not evidence that a public audience or institution attributed autonomous authority to Lean.

The primary *Science* paper reports denominators and human validation. The case may be a mixed test of how formal benchmark curation, human statement validation, and public headlines interact. The manuscript needs matched primary quotations and source-by-source analysis.

**Current judgement:** useful for the boundary between artifact validity and public attribution, but not yet a strong case of fetishism.

### 4.5 Liquid Tensor: strong counter-case, but not a matched control

Liquid Tensor's explicit contributor acknowledgements and definition sanity checks make it a valuable counter-case. Yet it differs from the other cases in age, research setting, human-led production, and public communication.

The manuscript should avoid treating it as a controlled comparison. It is a theoretically relevant negative case, not a matched control.

**Current judgement:** strong evidence that Lean's authority need not be represented as autonomous; it does not by itself prove the causal role of visibility of collective labour.

## 5. Major issue IV: the literature positioning is incomplete after new 2026 work

The manuscript acknowledges Hourigan (2009), Omodeo (2019), Hardwig (1985), De Millo et al. (1979), Kallel and El Louadi (2026), and Duede and Davey (2025). This is a good foundation. However, the literature review needs to incorporate at least three particularly relevant 2026 contributions located in the present review.

### 5.1 Vallverdú (2026): *Where Does Mathematical Warrant Live?*

Jordi Vallverdú, “Where Does Mathematical Warrant Live? From the Four-Color Theorem to Mass-Released AI ‘Results’,” *Philosophies* 11(6), article 224 (2026), DOI: 10.3390/philosophies11060224.

This paper argues that surveyability, transferability, reliability, and explanatory power should not be predicated of a proof as a whole without specifying the components of its warrant architecture. Its proposed Locus Thesis allocates warrant across the intended claim, object-level statement, justificatory object, checking procedure, trusted base, error profile, epistemic-function allocation, independent replication routes, and selection profile.

This is directly relevant to the manuscript's five-level account and its claim that local verification should not be generalized to the whole proof. The paper must cite it and explain the difference. The current five-level framework cannot be presented as a novel contribution.

### 5.2 López-Corona (2026): *From Authorial Mathematics to Studio Mathematics*

Oliver López-Corona, “From Authorial Mathematics to Studio Mathematics: Ecobiontic Forms of Proof after Large Language Models,” arXiv:2608.22194 (2026).

This preprint explicitly analyzes human-machine assemblages, proof assistants, formal libraries, provenance, mathematical understanding, human epistemic agency, and institutional responsibility. It discusses Polymath, Liquid Tensor, and contemporary AI-assisted mathematics.

This is especially close to the manuscript's account of collective epistemic labour. The paper should explain why a Žižekian analysis of the symbolic autonomy attributed to the machine adds something beyond López-Corona's governance and assemblage framework.

### 5.3 Erfani (2026): *Not “just a language model”: AI's ideological functions*

Farhang Erfani, “Not ‘just a language model’: AI's ideological functions,” *AI & Society* 41 (2026): 869–881, DOI: 10.1007/s00146-025-02623-0.

This paper directly studies AI systems' ideological functions and uses concepts including infrastructure, normalization, subjectification, opacity, and pastiche. It is broader and focused on LLMs, but it means the manuscript must not imply that ideology critique of AI systems is an unoccupied field.

### 5.4 The originality claim must be narrowed

Hourigan already applies Žižek to technological fetishization and knowledge production. Erfani analyzes AI's ideological functions. López-Corona analyzes the human-machine assemblage in mathematics. Vallverdú develops a component-level warrant architecture for AI-assisted and computer-assisted proof.

The manuscript's plausible contribution is therefore not “the first Žižekian account of machine authority” or “the first account of formal verification's social epistemology.”

A more defensible contribution is:

> A Žižekian analysis of the symbolic function of the Lean-verification credential, distinguishing the genuine warrant supplied by a formal checker from the possible objectal displacement and social legitimation through which that credential is made to stand for a wider mathematical claim.

This contribution remains promising, but it needs to be demonstrated through the cases rather than asserted in the introduction.

## 6. Major issue V: manuscript-level scholarly apparatus needs substantial work

### 6.1 Primary-text citations are too coarse

The manuscript cites *The Sublime Object of Ideology* by broad page ranges, but several claims require more exact textual support. In particular:
- the move from commodity fetishism to objectal autonomy;
- the function of fantasy in sustaining social reality;
- the distinction between cynical knowledge and practical ideological investment;
- the relation between the Big Other and the lack of a final guarantee.

The paper should quote or closely paraphrase the relevant passages with exact page references from the edition actually used. The familiar “they know very well what they are doing, yet they are doing it” formula should be attributed carefully to Sloterdijk's account of cynical reason as mediated through Žižek, not presented as Žižek's original phrase.

The manuscript also needs to avoid using the claim “a true proposition can function ideologically” as if it were a direct quotation or self-sufficient Žižekian theorem. It is an interpretive summary that needs argument.

### 6.2 The case citations are incomplete

The reference list contains incomplete entries:
- “Tsoukalas, et al.” should be replaced by the full author list or a journal-approved shortened format.
- The Science paper's DOI, volume, issue, and pages/article details should be verified.
- The Science Media Centre expert reactions are listed without a precise page title or URL.
- The Riemann exposition needs a full Zenodo citation and version identifier.
- The OpenAI announcement, repository commit, independent critique, and response chain should be cited separately.
- The Lean community and Topaz materials should be cited with exact titles and dates.

The primary case claims need in-text citations at the point where they are made. A reference list at the end does not make the empirical narrative adequately auditable.

### 6.3 The paper needs a transparent source-selection method

The manuscript describes a “source-based comparative analysis” but does not say how the sources were selected. It should state:
- the evidence cut-off date;
- which source types were included;
- how official statements, repositories, independent critiques, institutional reports, and media coverage were paired;
- how contrary evidence and corrections were retained;
- why these four cases were selected;
- what the study does not infer from public texts.

This need not become a large qualitative-methods section. A concise and reproducible method statement is sufficient.

### 6.4 There is reference-list duplication

Omodeo and Hourigan appear in the main reference list and again after the “Primary case records” heading. They should appear once in the bibliography. The primary case records should either be integrated into one reference list or clearly separated without duplication.

## 7. Minor and editorial issues

1. The title refers to “scientific knowledge production,” while all four empirical cases concern mathematical proof and mathematical research. Either justify the broader scope or narrow the title to mathematical knowledge production.
2. The term “formal verification” should be defined once and used consistently. Do not slide between “formal proof,” “machine verification,” “proof assistant,” and “AI verification” as if they were equivalent.
3. “Scientific certainty” is too broad in some passages. Prefer “mathematical warrant,” “proof-level assurance,” or “institutional recognition” where appropriate.
4. The five epistemic levels are useful, but their relation to Vallverdú's warrant architecture and Kallel–El Louadi's three layers must be made explicit.
5. The manuscript should not imply that every public account is addressed to a general audience; identify the audience and genre of each source.
6. The discussion repeats the same central distinction several times. Once the technical warrant and ideological extension are carefully defined, later sections can be shorter.
7. The Big Other section is useful as background but currently occupies disproportionate space relative to the three main mechanisms. Keep it concise and subordinate to the Žižekian analysis of fantasy and fetishism.
8. The article should avoid using “ideological” as a synonym for “socially mediated.” Social mediation is ubiquitous; ideology requires a more specific account of the function of the representation.

## 8. Recommended revision plan

The following is a bounded revision plan. These are the changes that materially affect publishability.

### Priority 1 — Rebuild the central theoretical inference
- Identify the specific lack or antagonism that the fantasy of verification organizes.
- Define the fetishized object precisely: preferably the credential “Lean-verified” or the machine-checking result as an objectal bearer of authority.
- Distinguish objectal displacement from mere omission of human contributors.
- Make disavowal optional and secondary.
- Explain the difference between a Žižekian ideological function and ordinary rhetorical overstatement or institutional legitimation.

### Priority 2 — Repair the diagnostic criteria
- Treat technical warrant as the baseline, not a discriminating ideology indicator.
- Keep scope enlargement and fetishistic displacement analytically separate.
- Either substantiate social reproduction or revise R into a social-efficacy criterion with evidence requirements.
- Do not claim that any current case meets the full criterion unless the matched sources demonstrate it.

### Priority 3 — Reframe the four cases
- Riemann: formal certification, AI-mediated exposition, expert understanding, and institutional recognition.
- Navier–Stokes: exact scope comparison between the Lean declarations, the written proof, the announcement, and subsequent responses.
- Erdős: benchmark scope, statement identity, human validation, and artifact provenance.
- Liquid Tensor: counter-case of visible semantic checks and collective practice.
- Treat the cases as heterogeneous comparative configurations, not four repetitions of one phenomenon.

### Priority 4 — Update literature and originality
- Add Vallverdú (2026), López-Corona (2026), and Erfani (2026).
- Explicitly distinguish the proposed Žižekian contribution from warrant architecture, human-machine assemblage/governance, and general AI ideology critique.
- Narrow the novelty claim.

### Priority 5 — Complete scholarly apparatus
- Verify exact primary-text pages.
- Complete in-text citations for every case-specific empirical claim.
- Complete bibliographic entries and remove duplicates.
- Add a concise source-selection and evidence protocol.

## 9. Final recommendation

**Major revision.**

The manuscript has a publishable question and a promising theoretical intuition, but its central concept is not yet sufficiently distinguished from ordinary scope inflation, rhetorical compression, and institutional legitimation. The four cases are useful but do not yet establish the full diagnostic conjunction proposed by the manuscript. The literature review also needs updating to include recent work on warrant architecture and human-machine mathematics.

The most promising route is not to broaden the paper. It is to narrow and deepen it: focus on the credential “Lean-verified” as a possible objectal bearer of epistemic authority; specify the social lack it helps manage; distinguish the formal warrant from its semantic and institutional extensions; and demonstrate the ideological function through matched primary-source comparisons.

If those revisions are made, the paper could make a distinctive contribution to philosophy of mathematical practice and the social epistemology of formal verification through a Žižekian critique of ideology. In its current form, I would not recommend acceptance or immediate submission.
