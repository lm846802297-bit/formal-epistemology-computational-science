# Primary-text derivation of the Žižekian model

**Project:** *In Lean We Trust? The Ideology of Machine-Checked Proofs in Science*  
**Date:** 27 November 2026  
**Status:** source-grounded derivation map for the controlling model in THEORETICAL_MODEL.md. Page references are tied to the editions listed below and must be checked against the exact editions used in the final manuscript.

## 1. Reading decision

The model is derived primarily from two Žižek texts:

1. *The Sublime Object of Ideology* (1989): the principal source for fetishism, ideological fantasy, and cynical ideology.
2. “The Spectre of Ideology” (1994): the principal source for ideology beyond false consciousness, the possibility that true statements function ideologically, and the three planes of ideology.

Marx and Lacan are theoretical sources internal to Žižek's framework. They are not co-equal theoretical centres of the paper. Hardwig remains a normative comparator for justified epistemic dependence.

There is no need to turn the project into a general survey of all Žižek's writings. *The Plague of Fantasies* and *For They Know Not What They Do* may be consulted for further elaboration if a precise argument requires them; they are not prerequisites for the minimal model.

## 2. Primary source I — Žižek, *The Sublime Object of Ideology* (1989)

Edition: Slavoj Žižek, *The Sublime Object of Ideology* (London: Verso, 1989). Page references below refer to the first English edition; pagination may differ in later reprints.

### 2.1 Fetishism: pp. 19–20

In Chapter 1, “How Did Marx Invent the Symptom?”, Žižek's discussion of commodity fetishism focuses attention on the form through which an effect generated by a network of social relations appears as an immediate property of an element within that network.

**Derivation for this paper:**
- The reliability of a Lean-checked result is real.
- The relevance and authority of the result depend on relations among theorem formulation, formalization, libraries, kernel/toolchain, semantic comparison, expert assessment, and institutions.
- The fetishistic hypothesis arises if the authority generated through these relations appears as an autonomous property of “Lean itself.”

The paper's term **the ideological fetishism of formal verification** is a proposed extension of this structure. It is not a term coined by Žižek. The analogy is structural, not an identity with the capitalist commodity form.

### 2.2 Cynicism and disavowal: pp. 24–27

Žižek's discussion of cynical ideology develops the point that ideology can persist after the relevant actors are no longer simply unaware of the facts. The familiar formula “they know very well what they are doing, yet they are doing it” is associated with Sloterdijk's critique of cynical reason; it should not be presented as Žižek's original coinage.

**Derivation for this paper:** acknowledged knowledge of the limits of Lean verification does not automatically prevent a credential from continuing to function as a broader guarantee. This is a hypothesis about documented practice, not a diagnosis of an individual's unconscious state.

### 2.3 Ideological fantasy: pp. 28–31, especially p. 30

Žižek's account of ideological fantasy moves beyond the idea that ideology is merely a false picture of reality. Fantasy supplies coordinates through which social reality is experienced and sustained. The illusion can operate in practical activity, not merely in the conscious propositions people endorse.

**Derivation for this paper:** the formal-verification event may be represented as a scene of closure: a complex process appears to culminate in the verdict “verified.” The local certainty of kernel checking may then symbolize a wider closure of uncertainty. This is not the claim that Lean literally checks empirical reality; it is a hypothesis about the symbolic role assigned to the local check.

### 2.4 The Big Other and the guarantee: pp. 78–80, 102–110, 136–138

These passages clarify the symbolic order, the lack in the Other, and the fantasy of a guarantee of meaning.

**Derivation for this paper:** Lean is not the Big Other. It may be positioned as a technical credential within a symbolic order that seeks a guarantee of scientific knowledge. The absence of a final guarantor is a structural background to the model, not a fourth co-equal mechanism.

## 3. Primary source II — Žižek, “The Spectre of Ideology” (1994)

Edition: Slavoj Žižek, “The Spectre of Ideology,” in *Mapping Ideology*, ed. Slavoj Žižek (London: Verso, 1994), pp. 1–33.

### 3.1 Ideology beyond false consciousness: approximately pp. 8–10

Žižek argues that the ideological status of a position cannot be read directly from the truth or falsity of its explicit content. A proposition may be factually accurate and nevertheless function ideologically, depending on its subjective position and the social relation it helps reproduce.

**Derivation:** the truth of the Lean check is not the issue under critique. The analysis asks what the check is made to authorize and whether a bounded formal warrant is used to legitimize a broader scientific claim.

### 3.2 The three planes of ideology: approximately pp. 9–10

Žižek discusses ideology through three connected planes:
1. doctrine or explicit ideas;
2. ideology in its material externality and apparatuses;
3. the more elusive spontaneous ideology operating at the heart of social reality.

He relates this reconstruction to the Hegelian triad of in-itself, for-itself, and in-and-for-itself. He also notes that Marx did not himself use the term “ideology” for commodity fetishism.

**Derivation:** the empirical study must not be limited to headlines or press-release language. It should inspect:
- explicit statements about what Lean verifies;
- material and institutional procedures that certify and circulate the result;
- repeated practices in which the credential is treated as a routine warrant.

These are empirical planes, not three additional theoretical mechanisms. Fantasy, fetishism, and disavowal can operate across them.

### 3.3 Cynicism: the true statement and its ideological function

Žižek's example of cynical ideology shows how an explicit admission can coexist with the continuation of a practice that serves a social relation. The point is not that every contradiction is cynical; the relation between acknowledged knowledge and practice must be demonstrated.

**Derivation:** an explicit statement that Lean's scope is limited, followed by an overextended claim, is a lead for investigation—not sufficient proof of cynical disavowal.

## 4. Marx and Lacan as sources within Žižek's framework

### 4.1 Marx: *Capital*, Volume I, Chapter 1, Section 4

Karl Marx, *Capital*, vol. I, ch. 1, §4, “The Fetishism of Commodities and the Secret Thereof.”

Marx's account explains how a definite social relation among people assumes the fantastic form of a relation among things. Žižek's reading is the source for the paper's emphasis on the appearance of a network-generated effect as an intrinsic property of one object.

**Limit:** Lean is not a commodity in the precise Marxian sense. The paper borrows the structure of displacement, not the full economic analysis of exchange-value.

### 4.2 Lacan: the absence of a final guarantee

Jacques Lacan, *Desire and Its Interpretation: The Seminar of Jacques Lacan, Book VI*, ed. Jacques-Alain Miller, trans. Bruce Fink (Cambridge: Polity, 2019), Chapter XVI, “There Is No Other of the Other,” pp. 291–305.

This is the primary Lacanian source for the absence of a final symbolic guarantor. It supports the claim that no higher signifier can guarantee the consistency of the symbolic order from outside.

**Limit:** the paper does not identify Lean with the Big Other. Nor does it make the subject-supposed-to-know a central explanatory mechanism. Lacan supports the Žižekian account of fantasy and guarantee; he is not a parallel theoretical centre.

## 5. Disavowal: exact attribution and operational use

Žižek's primary texts establish the importance of cynical ideology and the persistence of ideological practice beyond simple ignorance. The more explicit distinction between cynical and fetishistic disavowal is sharpened in later scholarship:

- Jack Black, “Examining the Mechanism of Disavowal and its Two Forms: Cynical Disavowal and Fetishistic Disavowal,” *Theory & Psychology* 35(1) (2025), 117–135. DOI: 10.1177/09593543241308199.
- Tereza Østbø Kuldova, “Fetishism and the Problem of Disavowal,” *Qualitative Market Research* (2019). DOI: 10.1108/QMR-12-2016-0125.

For this paper:
- **Fetishistic disavowal** is the hypothesis that a limitation is acknowledged while the verification object continues to sustain the broader symbolic investment.
- **Cynical disavowal** is the stricter hypothesis that a manipulative distance obscures the gap between acknowledged knowledge and practice.

The paper must not attribute Black's explicit two-form taxonomy to Žižek as if Žižek had formulated it in exactly that form. Nor may either form be inferred from one headline or from the mere fact that researchers trust Lean.

## 6. From the primary texts to the model

The derivation is:

1. **Beyond false consciousness:** ideology cannot be identified simply with factual error. A true local claim can function ideologically through the role it plays in a wider social relation.
2. **Fantasy:** a bounded formal check can be staged as a scene of closure that makes broader scientific certainty appear attainable.
3. **Fetishism:** the network of human, technical, semantic, and institutional relations that makes the check meaningful can appear as an autonomous property of the machine.
4. **Disavowal:** when the relevant limits are known, the symbolic investment may nevertheless persist. This is a conditional, higher-evidence hypothesis.
5. **Missing guarantee:** no technical or institutional credential can supply an ultimate guarantee of every level of scientific knowledge.
6. **Three planes:** the analysis examines explicit discourse, material-institutional practice, and repeated spontaneous reliance—not only public wording.

This yields the central model:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{Genuine, bounded technical warrant}\\
&+\text{collective epistemic mediation}\\
&+\text{institutional and symbolic circulation}\\
&\Downarrow\\
&\text{Fantasy of closure}
+\text{fetishistic displacement}\\
&\Downarrow\\
&\text{Possible ideological enlargement of the verification credential}\\
&\Downarrow\\
&\text{Disavowal, if acknowledged limits coexist with continued investment.}
\end{aligned}
\]

Fantasy and fetishism are mutually reinforcing dimensions, not a mechanical sequence. Disavowal is conditional, not a necessary stage.

## 7. Prior research and originality boundary

- **Hourigan (2009):** already applies Žižek to technological fetishization, fantasy, and knowledge production. The paper cannot claim to be the first Žižekian critique of technology.
- **Omodeo (2019):** establishes a broader political epistemology of ideology in science studies. Our object is narrower: the symbolic authority of formal verification.
- **Flisfeder (2023):** provides a contemporary account of Žižek's Hegelian-Lacanian critique of ideology, the Big Other, cynicism, and commodity fetishism.
- **Black (2025) and Kuldova (2019):** refine the distinction between cynical and fetishistic disavowal.
- **Ahlstrom-Vij (2021):** uses “epistemic fetishism” in a different analytic-epistemological sense. The project therefore uses the full expression “the ideological fetishism of formal verification” and does not claim to have coined the bare phrase “epistemic fetishism.”
- **Kallel and El Louadi (2026):** already distinguish derivational validity, representational fidelity, and epistemic significance. These distinctions are prior work, not our novelty.
- **Duede and Davey (2025):** defend the genuine epistemic value of transparent proof checking, providing an important counterweight to any claim that machine verification is merely ideological.

## 8. Final reading priority

The minimum primary-text reading for the model is:
1. *The Sublime Object of Ideology*, pp. 19–31 and the selected Big Other passages.
2. “The Spectre of Ideology,” pp. 1–33.
3. Marx, *Capital*, vol. I, ch. 1, §4.
4. Lacan, *Seminar VI*, ch. XVI, pp. 291–305.

The remaining Žižek texts are supplementary. The next research step is to test the derived model against the four Lean cases, not to expand the bibliography without a specific conceptual need.
