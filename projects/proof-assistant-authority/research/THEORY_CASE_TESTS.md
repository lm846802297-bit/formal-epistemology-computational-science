# Testing the Žižekian theory against the four Lean cases

**Project:** *In Lean We Trust? The Ideology of Machine-Checked Proofs in Science*  
**Date:** 27 November 2026  
**Status:** first theory-to-evidence test; provisional, based on the current source audit. This is not a final mathematical referee report or a representative study of public opinion.

## 1. Test design

The three mechanisms are treated as distinct hypotheses:
- **Fantasy of verification:** does the public or institutional narrative make a bounded Lean check appear to close scientific uncertainty more generally?
- **Epistemic fetishism:** does the public representation make the authority generated by collective human, semantic, and institutional labour appear as an autonomous property of Lean?
- **Big Other / missing guarantee:** is Lean positioned as a credential within a symbolic order that behaves as if a final guarantor of scientific knowledge were available, despite no such final guarantor existing?

A case supports a mechanism only when the evidence shows the relevant representational or institutional structure. Technical checking by itself does not establish ideology. Public texts establish invitations to trust and forms of representation; they do not by themselves establish what the public believes or any individual's unconscious motives.

## 2. Riemann hypothesis — Claude + Lean

### What is actually established
- Alpöge's 20 November 2026 note presents the claim as a discovery made while working with Claude and releases a Lean development.
- The original archive and subsequent compact rewrite have extensive reported checking: full compilation/kernel replay, independent proof-checker runs, and comparison of the formal statement with the Mathlib statement.
- The 26 November public status note says that no mathematician has read the proof through and that there is no conventional mathematical paper accompanying the archive. This does not show the proof is false; it distinguishes machine verification from human mathematical understanding.
- Expert commentary in the UK Science Media Centre on 24 November explicitly distinguishes successful checking of code from establishing that the formal statement is the Riemann hypothesis as understood by mathematicians, and from understanding the proof's mathematical structure.

### Fantasy test
**Evidence in favour:** a historically unresolved problem is announced alongside a very large Lean artifact and multiple checker results. This creates a highly visible scene in which a complex mathematical question appears to receive a reproducible machine verdict.

**Evidence against / qualification:** the primary statement is phrased as the author's belief about a discovery; the Lean/Mathlib community and expert commentators distinguish formal certification from human understanding and conventional acceptance. These qualifications resist a simple “the machine settled everything” narrative.

**Provisional result:** plausible symbolic staging of certainty, but no evidence that the public uniformly interprets the check as final mathematical acceptance.

### Fetishism test
**Evidence in favour:** some compressed public descriptions foreground “Claude + Lean” as the agents that solved RH, which can background Alpöge's mathematical direction, the formalization work, Mathlib, checker developers, and independent human assessment.

**Evidence against:** the Waterloo and Lean community records name contributors and explain the verification infrastructure; expert reporting explicitly identifies the distinction between machine checking and mathematical exposition.

**Provisional result:** possible in selected headlines, not established as the dominant public representation. The collective network remains visible in primary technical accounts.

### Big Other test
Lean's multiple independent checks may function as a credential that supports the social claim that the result is reliable. But the same public record shows that this credential does not settle human understanding, peer review, or Clay recognition.

**Provisional result:** Lean can be positioned as a symbolic credential, but the case also exposes the absence of a final guarantee. It is an ambivalent case rather than a simple confirmation of fetishism.

## 3. Navier–Stokes — OpenAI + Lean

### What is actually established
- OpenAI published a natural-language proof claim and a Lean formalization for a finite-time blow-up result for three-dimensional incompressible Navier–Stokes with smooth forcing.
- The repository's Lean declarations can be checked by Lean's kernel; the project metadata records the formalization and its self-assessed review status.
- Bastounis, Circelli, and Hansen (arXiv:2610.08144) report discrepancies between the natural-language paper and Lean formalization, including the derivative count in the inverse estimate of Lemma 8.6 and a difference in the pressure-flux estimate/proof route.
- The independent critique explicitly concerns non-faithful translation between proof artifacts. It does not establish that the Lean kernel accepted an invalid proof or that the underlying Navier–Stokes theorem is false. OpenAI's response, as reported by New Scientist, acknowledges a mismatch and says it does not by itself show either proof invalid.

### Fantasy test
**Evidence in favour:** an announcement of a solution to a Millennium Prize problem accompanied by Lean verification can be read as a complete, machine-certified settlement.

**Evidence against / qualification:** the later critique and institutional responses publicly distinguish the formalized declarations from the written paper. The public record therefore contains a contest over the scope of the guarantee rather than a settled social belief in a final verdict.

**Provisional result:** strongest candidate for an enlarged fantasy of verification, because the phrase “Lean-verified solution” can appear to settle more than the formal object alone settles. Need a matched analysis of the original announcement, its qualifications, and later corrections before coding any particular statement as substantive scope expansion.

### Fetishism test
**Evidence in favour:** the kernel's authority over formal declarations can stand in for the semantic correspondence between those declarations and the separate natural-language proof. The formal check risks appearing as an autonomous guarantee even though the link to the intended claim requires human mathematical judgment.

**Evidence against:** the formal repository, independent critique, and OpenAI response are public; the mismatch is not wholly concealed. This visibility complicates any claim of systematic disavowal.

**Provisional result:** strongest of the four cases for the proposed epistemic-fetishism mechanism at the level of representation. The claim is about the possible social displacement of semantic mediation, not about a kernel failure.

### Big Other test
The public status of a Millennium-level result is not determined by Lean alone. The Clay Institute, mathematical experts, publication, and community acceptance remain separate authorizing sites. The dispute shows that no single checker can act as a final guarantor for every level of the claim.

**Provisional result:** strong case for analyzing the desire for a final technical guarantor and the limits of that guarantee.

## 4. Erdős problems — AlphaProof Nexus + Lean

### What is actually established
- Tsoukalas et al. report 9 solutions among 353 Erdős problems already represented as formal Lean statements and 44 proofs among 492 OEIS conjectures attempted.
- The *Science* paper describes Lean-based proof search and reports human expert validation of the correspondence between the nine formal Erdős statements and the intended conjectures; OEIS formalizations were manually reviewed.
- Science Media Centre Spain expert reactions are mixed: they include enthusiasm about AI-assisted discovery, questions about the value/cost of the more elaborate agent, concerns about statement fidelity, and concerns about certified results that are not fully understood.
- Carlos Toledo's secondary audit reports that proofs replay under two kernels but raises questions about historical novelty and interpretation of some formal statements. The auditor limits its own conclusions and does not settle every statement's relation to the original conjectures.

### Fantasy test
**Evidence in favour:** public headlines such as “AI solves Erdős problems” can suggest autonomous mathematical discovery and a broad capability beyond the bounded formalized benchmark.

**Evidence against:** the primary paper reports denominators, formal statement selection, Lean checking, and human validation. These details resist a generalized claim that AI has solved mathematics as a whole.

**Provisional result:** the fantasy is more likely to occur in compressed headlines than in the full research account. The case is a useful test of whether media compression preserves or enlarges scope.

### Fetishism test
**Evidence in favour:** if the public attributes the achievements solely to AlphaProof Nexus, it may obscure the role of formal conjecture curation, Lean, human statement validation, and expert review.

**Evidence against:** the *Science* article makes the workflow and human checks relatively visible. The independent audit also shows that public authority is contestable and distributed.

**Provisional result:** limited support; stronger as a counter-case demonstrating that transparent mediation can resist machine-autonomy narratives.

### Big Other test
The result's authority is distributed across the AI system, Lean, the Formal Conjectures repository, expert validation, the journal, and the mathematical community. The publication is not a final guarantee that every historical interpretation or novelty judgment is settled.

**Provisional result:** a mediated-authority case rather than a clear instance of a machine acting as a final symbolic guarantor.

## 5. Liquid Tensor Experiment — historical Lean case

### What is actually established
- The Lean community's 15 July 2022 completion announcement states that the main theorem of liquid vector spaces was formally verified on 14 July and identifies the formal statement and contributors.
- The project record credits mathematicians, formalizers, Mathlib dependencies, and institutional support.
- Scholze's June 2021 account describes a partial milestone and emphasizes the human mathematical interaction and scrutiny that produced his confidence; his comment explicitly resists attributing that confidence to the computer alone.
- Quanta's 2021 coverage foregrounds Lean's role but also reports that the formalized part was the part Scholze was most concerned about; the article should be coded in its full context rather than from its headline alone.

### Fantasy test
**Evidence in favour:** a public headline about Lean confirming a proof can create an image of technical closure.

**Evidence against:** the primary completion announcement is specific about the theorem and contributors, and Scholze's account makes human scrutiny central.

**Provisional result:** weak-to-moderate candidate; primary sources largely resist the fantasy of a self-sufficient machine verdict.

### Fetishism test
**Evidence in favour:** a headline that attributes confirmation to Lean may background the collective project.

**Evidence against:** the official project account explicitly names the mathematical and technical contributors and the supporting infrastructure.

**Provisional result:** weak support. This is an important counter-case to any thesis that Lean verification is inherently fetishistic.

### Big Other test
The authority is visibly hybrid: the formal theorem, expert mathematicians, formalization community, and Mathlib all contribute. Scholze's confidence is linked to human scrutiny, not simply to a machine verdict.

**Provisional result:** evidence that symbolic authority can be supported by collective practice rather than concentrated in the machine.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Riemann | Plausible but qualified | Possible in compressed reporting | Lean as credential, not final guarantor | Formal certainty vs human understanding |

## 7. What the cases do not yet establish

- No case establishes that the general public uniformly treats Lean as a final guarantor of scientific truth.
- No case establishes cynical ideology in the strict sense. Evidence of acknowledged limitations plus continued practical reproduction of an enlarged guarantee is not yet sufficient.
- Public texts and expert comments establish representations and attributed positions, not unconscious motives.
- A claim that a headline materially changes public belief requires audience evidence; the Chen–Jia experiment concerns AI disclosure generally, not Lean proof verification.

## 8. Provisional theoretical conclusion

The four cases do not support the claim that Lean is inherently ideological or that machine verification necessarily produces fetishism. They support a more precise thesis:

**Lean can function as a symbolic credential of scientific reliability. Its ideological function depends on how the bounded formal check is connected to semantic interpretation, collective labour, institutional recognition, and public communication.**

The Navier–Stokes case is the strongest candidate for epistemic fetishism; Riemann is the strongest case for the coexistence of high technical certification and incomplete human understanding; Erdős and Liquid Tensor provide important counter-cases in which mediation is more visible.
## 9. Source-level update (27 November 2026) — supersedes earlier case judgments where inconsistent

### Riemann
The Lean FRO/Mathlib joint statement of 21 November explicitly certifies the statement and proof after expert vetting of the Mathlib statement, compilation, independent statement comparison, and multiple proof checkers. A 1,582-page exposition (rh-lean-roadmap v44, 26 November) now exists; several AI models wrote it from the Lean source and cross-checked it. It is inaccurate to describe the case as lacking a readable mathematical account. The more precise issue is how formal verification, institutional certification, and AI-mediated exposition combine, and how these differ from independent human mathematical understanding.

### Navier–Stokes
Source-level inspection confirms the exact m+4/m+5 derivative discrepancy in the pinned Lean declaration norm_derivativeWord_inverse_le. It also confirms that the cited pressure-flux declaration contains an A_R/R + 1/R^2 term and uses a different proof route from the natural-language Eq. (10.19). The critique says the pressure-flux bounds are difficult to compare directly and does not establish the theorem false. The strongest ideological issue is the possibility that a valid Lean proof of formal declarations is represented as validating the whole written paper.

### Erdős
Static source comparison of the pinned AlphaProof result files with the paper's prose shows specific mismatches: #26's prose uses an upper-density bound below 3/4, while the linked Lean file formalizes a lower-density Tenenbaum variant; #741(i)'s prose states an upper-density theorem, while the linked Lean file proves a negative answer for an exact-density variant. A separate upper-density Lean proof exists in a later Formal Conjectures commit. #125's linked file matches the positive-lower-density formal variant but is not the same file as the original forum proof. These are artifact/prose correspondence issues, not kernel failures. The independent Erdős Problems audit is static; this project fetched the files but did not rebuild them.

### Liquid Tensor
Topaz's 14 October 2022 post explicitly addresses whether the formal definitions are correct and documents readable sanity-check examples for mathematicians with minimal Lean experience. This strengthens the case as a counter-example to autonomous machine authority.
### Additional #741(ii) provenance finding

The Problem 741 page and static audit identify the correct #741(ii) formalization in Formal Conjectures. Together with the #741(i) exact-density/upper-density mismatch, this means both part-specific Lean links in the preprint are problematic.
