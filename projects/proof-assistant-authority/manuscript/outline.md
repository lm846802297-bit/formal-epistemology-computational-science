# In Lean We Trust? — Manuscript outline

**Subtitle:** The Ideology of Machine-Checked Proofs in Science

1. **Introduction:** the contemporary rise of AI-assisted mathematical discovery and machine-checked proofs; why their authority matters beyond scientific communities, for society's trust in scientific knowledge.
2. **Epistemic dependence and public trust:** Hardwig, expert testimony, collective epistemic labour, institutional trust, and the ordinary public's rational dependence on science.
3. **Žižek's critique of ideology:** ideological fantasy, fetishism, cynical ideology, and the Big Other. Clarify the distinct role of Lacan's subject-supposed-to-know.
4. **From formal correctness to public authority:** distinguish technical verification, semantic correspondence, mathematical understanding, institutional recognition, and public trust.
5. **Four contemporary case studies:** Riemann hypothesis (Claude + Lean); Waring's problem (GPT-6.2 Astra); Navier–Stokes (OpenAI multi-agent discovery + Lean); Erdős problems (AlphaProof Nexus). For each, compare the formal artifact, AI contribution, human review, institutional status, media framing, and public reception.
6. **Society-wide reception:** analyze population surveys, news coverage, and a bounded qualitative sample of public discussion. Distinguish representative survey evidence from self-selected online discourse.
7. **Discussion:** determine whether the evidence supports ideological fantasy, epistemic fetishism, cynical ideology, legitimate epistemic dependence, ordinary compression, institutional authority, or hybrid explanations. Do not infer unconscious motives or population-wide beliefs from isolated texts.
8. **Conclusion:** explain how machine verification can become a symbol of scientific reliability while preserving the distinction between what is technically checked and what society is invited to believe.
