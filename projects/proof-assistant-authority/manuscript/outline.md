# Essayistic manuscript structure — *In Lean We Trust?*

**Title:** *In Lean We Trust? The Ideology of Machine-Checked Proofs in Science*  
**Current essayistic draft:** `IN_LEAN_WE_TRUST_ESSAYISTIC_DRAFT.md`  
**Date:** 27 November 2026

## Writing principle

The article is a philosophical argument with comparative case analysis, not an IMRaD-style empirical report. It begins with a paradox, develops the conceptual problem through dialectical argument, introduces Žižek where the problem requires him, and lets the four cases complicate rather than merely illustrate the theory.

The prose should remain academically precise, but the order of presentation is governed by the argument rather than by a fixed social-science template. Methodological safeguards and S–O–E diagnostic requirements remain present without dominating the main text.

## Structure

1. **The Paradox of a Verified Proof**  
   Begin with the question: what could be ideological about a correct proof? Establish the paradox that genuine technical reliability may make a wider symbolic investment possible.

2. **The Limits of Formal Certainty**  
   Explain Lean’s kernel-level warrant, the relation between intended claim and formal proposition, the component-based warrant architecture, and the distinction between rational epistemic dependence and fetishism.

3. **Žižek and the Ideological Structure of Verification**  
   Establish the theoretical grammar: ideology beyond false consciousness; fantasy as the staging of closure around a non-totalizable warrant relation; fetishism as the appearance of relation-dependent authority in object form; disavowal as a secondary mechanism. Fantasy explains the scene of closure; fetishism explains the apparent location of its authority.

4. **The Machine as a Fetish Object**  
   Develop the article’s own account of how epistemic fetishism forms through real technical reliability, distributed epistemic labour, credential condensation, and objectal displacement. Establish the fifth-stage finding: fetishistic disavowal does not require concealment of human mediation; it can operate through the public acknowledgement of that mediation while the machine remains the apparent autonomous bearer of mathematical authority. Use AlphaProof Nexus as the positive existence case.

5. **Four Encounters with Formal Verification**  
   - Riemann: composite institutional certification, with the warrant network explicitly visible.
   - Navier–Stokes: distinguish theorem-statement fidelity from proof-route correspondence; do not infer actor-level disavowal from an external critique.
   - Erdős / AlphaProof Nexus: the positive existence case for public-discourse fetishistic disavowal, where explicit human validation and natural-language exposition coexist with continued autonomous-machine framing.
   - Liquid Tensor: semantic risks are explicitly acknowledged and answered through public sanity checks, providing a counter-practice.

6. **The Ideological Function of Mathematical Certainty**  
   Compare how the five moments appear across the cases. Use AlphaProof Nexus to establish the existential case for fetishistic disavowal in public institutional representation; distinguish operational proof-search autonomy from the symbolic attribution of the whole mathematical achievement. The fifth-stage finding is that acknowledgement of human mediation does not necessarily dissolve symbolic investment in machine autonomy. The central finding is that public acknowledgement of human mediation does not necessarily dissolve symbolic investment in machine autonomy. Then locate the contribution against Vallverdú, Kallel–El Louadi, López-Corona, Erfani, Hourigan, and Omodeo.

7. **Conclusion**  
   Return to the opening paradox and state the final philosophical judgement.

## Non-negotiable analytical safeguards

- The kernel’s actual technical warrant is stated precisely.
- The formal proposition is distinguished from the intended mathematical claim.
- Social mediation is not automatically equated with ideology.
- A scope mismatch is not automatically objectal displacement.
- Objectal displacement is not automatically social efficacy.
- Disavowal is not inferred without actor-level evidence.
- The four cases are heterogeneous encounters, not matched controls or a representative sample.
- No case is claimed to meet the full S ∧ O ∧ E diagnosis unless all three elements are independently supported.
