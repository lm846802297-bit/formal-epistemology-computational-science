# Theoretical chapters — In Lean We Trust?

**Subtitle:** The Ideology of Machine-Checked Proofs in Science  
**Status:** Three substantive research drafts; not yet final journal prose. The chapter arguments have been checked against the primary-text locator file and literature review.

## Chapter 3.1 — The Ideological Fantasy of Scientific Certainty

[Open chapter draft](theory/3_1_IDEOLOGICAL_FANTASY.md)

Central argument: a bounded, reproducible Lean check may become a public scene in which the uncertainty of specialized knowledge appears to be closed. The fantasy is not that the kernel's result is false; it is that a real local guarantee may be invested with a wider symbolic function.

## Chapter 3.2 — From Commodity Fetishism to the Epistemic Fetishism of Formal Verification

[Open chapter draft](theory/3_2_EPISTEMIC_FETISHISM.md)

Central argument: the authority of a Lean-checked result depends on collective mathematical, formalization, software, expert, and institutional labour. Epistemic fetishism is our proposed extension for cases in which this socially produced reliability appears as an autonomous property of the machine.

## Chapter 3.3 — The Big Other and the Missing Guarantee

[Open chapter draft](theory/3_3_BIG_OTHER_AND_MISSING_GUARANTEE.md)

Central argument: Lean is not the Big Other. It may function as a technical credential within a symbolic order that seeks a final guarantor, although the Lacanian Big Other has no final external guarantee.

## Connected argument

1. Fantasy explains why a visible verification procedure can stage the closure of uncertainty.
2. Fetishism explains how collective epistemic labour can appear as a property of the machine.
3. The Big Other explains why the machine may be positioned as a guarantor within a symbolic order that has no final guarantor.

The common proposition is: **Verification does not abolish uncertainty; it relocates uncertainty.**
