# In Lean We Trust?
## The Ideology of Machine-Checked Proofs in Science

**Working title:** *In Lean We Trust? The Ideology of Machine-Checked Proofs in Science*

**Status:** independent follow-up project to the EJPS manuscript. The title and society-wide research scope are fixed. **Lean is the primary empirical object and the central proof-assistant case.** Other systems appear only as historical or conceptual comparators. The study does not claim a population-level causal effect or infer readers' unconscious motives from texts.

**Research question:** How does Lean-based machine verification become a publicly legible symbol of the reliability of scientific knowledge, and what ideological functions does this symbol perform in contemporary society?

**Research object:** not only mathematicians' trust in Lean, but the social chain through which AI-assisted mathematical results are produced, checked in Lean, appraised by experts and institutions, represented in public communication, and interpreted by ordinary audiences.

**Primary empirical cases:**
1. Riemann hypothesis — Claude-assisted discovery and extensive Lean verification.
2. Navier–Stokes — OpenAI multi-agent discovery, Lean formalization, and dispute about semantic correspondence with the written proof.
3. Erdős problems — AlphaProof Nexus, Lean proof search, formal conjecture statements, and human validation.
4. Liquid Tensor Experiment — historical Lean case on the relation between formal checking, expert scrutiny, and mathematical understanding.

**Contrast case:** Waring's problem — GPT-6.2 Astra-attributed mathematical preprint without a Lean certificate documented at the cut-off. It is used only to distinguish AI-generated mathematical claims from Lean-certified claims, not as a fifth co-equal Lean case.

**Theoretical orientation:** Žižek's critique of ideology is the principal philosophical lens, focusing on ideological fantasy, fetishism, and cynical ideology. Hardwig's epistemic dependence provides the social-epistemological baseline. Lacan's subject-supposed-to-know is a supporting account of the attribution of a knowing position, not a diagnosis of software or individual researchers.

**Relation to the EJPS paper:** The EJPS manuscript supplies a normative baseline for how a checked result can legitimately support a further claim. This project studies how Lean verification and its scope are represented across science and society. It does not revise the EJPS manuscript.
