# In Lean We Trust?
## The Ideology of Machine-Checked Proofs in Science

**Working title:** *In Lean We Trust? The Ideology of Machine-Checked Proofs in Science*

**Status:** initialized; independent follow-up project to the EJPS manuscript. The title is fixed as the working title. The empirical study remains in progress; no claim about readers' actual beliefs or authors' motives has been established.

**Research question:** How does the genuine local reliability of machine-checked proofs acquire broader epistemic and ideological authority in scientific knowledge production and communication?

**Theoretical orientation:** Žižek's critique of ideology is the principal philosophical lens, focusing on ideological fantasy, fetishism, and cynical ideology. Hardwig's epistemic dependence provides the social-epistemological baseline. Lacan's subject-supposed-to-know is a supporting account of the attribution of a knowing position, not a diagnosis of software or individual researchers.

**Empirical scope:** Public texts and formal artifacts from Lean, Coq, Isabelle/HOL, and related systems. The study compares theorem statements and formal project records with project-authored descriptions and independent retellings. It distinguishes accurate reporting, reasonable compression, ambiguity, and substantive scope expansion.

**Relation to the EJPS paper:** The EJPS manuscript supplies a normative baseline for how a checked result can legitimately support a further claim. This project studies how the scope of verification is represented and how authority is attributed in public discourse. It does not revise the EJPS manuscript or treat its schematic countermodel as behavioral evidence.
