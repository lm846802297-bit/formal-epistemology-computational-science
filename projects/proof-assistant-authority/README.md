# In Lean We Trust?
## The Ideology of Machine-Checked Proofs in Science

**Working title:** *In Lean We Trust? The Ideology of Machine-Checked Proofs in Science*

**Status:** independent follow-up project to the EJPS manuscript. The title and society-wide research scope are fixed. The four contemporary case studies are under evidence collection; no population-level causal claim or claim about readers' unconscious motives has been established.

**Research question:** How does machine verification become a publicly legible symbol of the reliability of scientific knowledge, and what ideological functions does this symbol perform in contemporary society?

**Research object:** not only scientists' or mathematicians' trust in proof assistants, but the social chain through which AI-assisted results are produced, checked, institutionally appraised, represented in public communication, and interpreted by ordinary audiences.

**Theoretical orientation:** Žižek's critique of ideology is the principal philosophical lens, focusing on ideological fantasy, fetishism, and cynical ideology. Hardwig's epistemic dependence provides the social-epistemological baseline. Lacan's subject-supposed-to-know is a supporting account of the attribution of a knowing position, not a diagnosis of software or individual researchers.

**Empirical scope:** four contemporary cases (Riemann hypothesis; Waring's problem; Navier–Stokes; Erdős problems), compared across AI contribution, verification, human/institutional review, media representation, and public reception. Historical proof-assistant cases remain controls. Population surveys provide context; online discussions are qualitative, self-selected material, not representative samples.

**Relation to the EJPS paper:** The EJPS manuscript supplies a normative baseline for how a checked result can legitimately support a further claim. This project studies how the scope of verification and the authority attached to it are represented across science and society. It does not revise the EJPS manuscript or treat its schematic countermodel as behavioral evidence.
