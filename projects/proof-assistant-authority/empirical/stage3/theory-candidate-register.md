## 2. Distinct explanatory tasks

- Marx explains why distributed social labour can appear as an autonomous property of the proof artifact.
- Bourdieu/Merton explain the conversion of public recognition into scientific credit and status.
- Derrida explains how archives, versioning and repeatability help stabilize an authoritative artifact.
- Lacan/Žižek explain the symbolic position assigned to the credential and the conditional fantasy of closure.
- Hardwig explains why reliance on experts and technical systems can be rational.
- STS explains the material practices and infrastructures through which proof artifacts are produced and maintained.

The manuscript should not become a list of philosophers. Each concept must perform a distinct task.

## 3. Evidential threshold for the Žižekian diagnosis

Tool use, kernel acceptance, institutional status or a public headline do not by themselves establish fetishistic disavowal.

For the stronger diagnosis, require:
1. an acknowledged constitutive gap;
2. an observable practice in which that gap is subordinated to the credential;
3. a consequence for recognition, attribution or subsequent action.

Stage 3 establishes localized credential-centred recognition and some public compression of formal targets into solved-status counts. It does not establish community-wide private disavowal. The next manuscript version should state the mechanism precisely rather than generalize it to all four cases.

## 4. Candidate original contribution

The candidate **post-verification circulation** model describes the ways a formally checked artifact may travel after kernel acceptance:

1. Credentialization — status, certification or solver attribution.
2. Translation — reconstruction as a human-readable mathematical argument.
3. Integration — import into a formal library, benchmark or toolchain.
4. Assimilation — use as a premise or object in subsequent mathematical research.
5. Archival stabilization — versioning, source pins, provenance and documentation.

The modes are not necessarily linear and can occur in parallel or independently. A case can have strong recognition and technical integration but little theorem-level assimilation. The four cases make this divergence visible.

The candidate concept is not presented as a priority claim. It must be positioned against prior work before publication.

## 5. Novelty constraints

- Kallel & El Louadi (2026) already distinguish derivational validity, representational fidelity and epistemic significance. Do not claim the three-layer distinction as new.
- López-Corona (2026) already studies studio mathematics, provenance, understanding audits and contribution-authority traces. Do not claim distributed human-machine authorship or LTE collaboration as new.
- Druskat (2019) and FORCE11 already address software/dependency citation, credit and provenance.
- De Millo, Lipton and Perlis, MacKenzie and Rosental already address the social assimilation and certification of proofs.
- Li et al. (2026) and Kurgan et al. (2026) already develop formal dependency networks and formal/informal theorem graphs.

The distinctive candidate contribution is the four-case comparison of how public recognition, human translation, technical integration and theorem-level assimilation diverge after formal verification, interpreted through a conditional account of symbolic guarantee and attribution.

## 7. Stage 3 final selection update

The final four-case comparison strengthens the conditional version of the framework and rules out a universal pipeline.

- Riemann demonstrates recognition, replay and reconstruction without located theorem-level dependence.
- Navier–Stokes demonstrates substantial technical artifact reuse while the paper-to-Lean intermediate correspondence remains contested.
- AlphaProof demonstrates that formal proof search can feed back into statement correction (#125 and #741), while the nine Erdős outputs remain distinct from the human-digested quantum-graph follow-up.
- LTE demonstrates explicit downstream uptake of semantic definition checks and Blueprint practice, while the original Lean 3 theorem code is not directly imported by the later projects inspected.

The most defensible contribution is therefore not a general theory of trust in proof assistants. It is a source-pinned account of how formal warrants acquire different afterlives—credentialization, translation, integration, assimilation or archival stabilization—and how those paths condition the conversion of bounded technical reliability into symbolic authority.

The core Marx–Lacan–Žižek framework is retained. The post-verification circulation model remains a descriptive middle-range contribution, not a substitute for the philosophical framework. Bourdieu/Merton, Derrida, Hardwig and STS retain the auxiliary roles specified in theory-synthesis.md.
