# Theory–Evidence Map

Version 0.1. Purpose: prevent theoretical claims from outrunning documentary evidence.

## 1. Technical distinctions

V = kernel acceptance of proof term for formal proposition P*.  
M = correspondence between P* and intended mathematical problem P.  
Pi = provenance and contribution history.  
S(P) = public status assigned to intended problem.

V = 1 does not by itself entail M = 1.  
V = 1 does not by itself entail appropriate credit attribution.

These are conceptual distinctions. Empirical research must establish how actual actors and institutions connect, separate or conflate them.

## 3. Case propositions to test

### Riemann
- Does the joint statement separate theorem-statement vetting, compilation reproduction and proof checking?
- Does the authority of the joint statement itself create a public status relied on beyond detailed grounds?
- Do critics challenge target, proof or social meaning of certification? Keep these distinct.

### Navier–Stokes
- Does the announcement distinguish formal theorem from broader Millennium Problem claim?
- What exactly do critiques challenge: derivation, statement correspondence, proof-route correspondence, assumptions or significance?
- How do public responses adjudicate these levels?
- Does the Lean certificate change uptake, or is it only associated with the public claim?

### AlphaProof Nexus
- Does “nine” mean nine original Erdős problems, nine formal statements, or a mixture including variants?
- Which exact records change status, and which statuses predate the agent result?
- How are agent, experts, formalizers and exposition authors credited?
- Is there downstream reuse, or only public recognition and independent replay?

### Liquid Tensor
- Does the examples folder make semantic correspondence more inspectable?
- Who actually uses these examples? Is external checking documented?
- How do Scholze's statements distribute epistemic agency?
- Do Blueprint and examples alter institutional recognition, or primarily improve the basis for trust?

## 4. Claims not to make without further evidence

1. “The mathematical community believes the machine solved the whole informal problem.” Requires direct evidence of uptake and target comparison.
2. “People know the credential is insufficient but use it anyway.” Requires actor-specific acknowledgement plus observable contrary practice.
3. “Human labour is erased.” Requires systematic comparison of labour with attribution; APN and LTE contain visible human roles.
4. “Transparency eliminates fetishism.” Not supported.
5. “The Big Other is Lean/GitHub/Mathlib.” These are institutions/infrastructures through which recognition is staged, not the Lacanian concept itself.
6. “A repository status equals consensus.” A merged PR is a localized institutional action.
7. “Independent kernel replay verifies the original problem.” It verifies the formal derivation; semantic correspondence is separate.
8. “Views, downloads, stars or news repetition demonstrate adoption.” Actual reuse or explicit reliance is required.

## 5. Minimum evidence rule

A central philosophical claim should normally have:
- one primary artifact or institutional record;
- one independent or participant source illuminating the practice;
- a traceable event/action, not only general opinion;
- a counter-evidence check;
- an explicit statement of what the evidence does not establish.

For claims about practical belief or fetishistic disavowal, require at least two distinct records: one documenting acknowledged limits and another documenting practical substitution or continued investment. A single headline is insufficient.
