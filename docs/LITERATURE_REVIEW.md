# First-Pass Literature Review (2026-11-26)

This is a structured first pass, not an exhaustive systematic review or basis for a priority claim.

## Machine-checked proof and mathematical knowledge
- **Duede & Davey (2025),** “A Priori Knowledge in an Era of Computational Opacity: The Role of Artificial Intelligence in Mathematical Discovery,” *Philosophy of Science* 92(5), 1394-1404, DOI 10.1017/psa.2025.10160. Transparent proof-checking can support mathematical knowledge despite opaque generation; the authors raise analogous possibilities for science. Our question concerns additional physical interpretation and model-target steps.
- **Avigad (2024),** “Mathematics and the Formal Turn,” *Bulletin of the AMS* 61(2), 225-240, DOI 10.1090/bull/1832. Background on formal mathematics.

## Proof correspondence and autoformalization
- **DeDeo & Duede (2026),** “A Correspondence Problem for Mathematical Proof,” *Philosophy of Science*, accepted/forthcoming; arXiv:2603.13680v2. Distinguishes adequate representation of the theorem from tracking the proof's inferential steps; discusses structural, causal, explanatory routes. Closest prior work: do not claim correspondence or proof tracking as new.
- **Meadows, Zhang & Freitas (2026),** “FormalScience: Scalable Human-in-the-Loop Autoformalisation of Science with Agentic Code Generation in Lean,” ACL 2026, 23059-23077, DOI 10.18653/v1/2026.acl-long.1057. Human-in-loop physics autoformalization, FormalPhysics dataset, semantic-drift taxonomy. Do not claim semantic drift as new.
- **Bastounis, Circelli & Hansen (2026),** “Navier-Stokes Lost in Translation,” arXiv:2610.08144v1. Preprint on why Lean acceptance does not guarantee faithful natural-language proof translation.

## Formalization of scientific theories
- **Bobbin et al. (2024),** “Formalizing Chemical Physics Using the Lean Theorem Prover,” *Digital Discovery* 3, 264-280, DOI 10.1039/D3DD00077J. Formalizes Langmuir/BET adsorption, thermodynamics, kinematics; authors distinguish formal derivation from empirical adequacy.
- **Douglas et al. (2026),** “Formalization of QFT,” arXiv:2603.15770v1. Lean formalization of a massive bosonic Euclidean field theory; distinguish this preprint version from later repository states.

## Models and computational trust
- **Imbert & Ardourel (2023),** “Formal Verification, Scientific Code, and the Epistemological Heterogeneity of Computational Science,” *Philosophy of Science* 90(2), 376-394, DOI 10.1017/psa.2022.78.
- **Graebner (2018),** “How to Relate Models to Reality?,” *JASSS* 21(3), DOI 10.18564/jasss.3772; application primarily agent-based modeling.
- **Knuuttila (2021),** “Epistemic Artifacts and the Modal Dimension of Modeling,” *European Journal for Philosophy of Science* 11, 65, DOI 10.1007/s13194-021-00374-5. Models can function as epistemic artifacts; literal representation is not always required.
- **Duran & Formanek (2018),** “Grounds for Trust: Essential Epistemic Opacity and Computational Reliabilism,” *Minds and Machines* 28, 645-666, DOI 10.1007/s11023-018-9481-6.

## OS primary literature and formal artifacts
Osterwalder & Schrader (1973), “Axioms for Euclidean Green's Functions,” CMP 31, 83-112, DOI 10.1007/BF01645738; and Osterwalder & Schrader (1975), “Axioms for Euclidean Green's Functions II,” CMP 42, 281-305, DOI 10.1007/BF01608978. Public formal artifacts and exact pins are analyzed in [the case study](CASE_STUDY_OS_RECONSTRUCTION.md).

## Provisional synthesis
Prior work substantially covers proof checking, correspondence, semantic drift, scientific formalization, model validation, and non-literal model representation. Candidate gap: an integrated physics-specific analysis of the interfaces connecting a checked theorem, interpreted model, concrete theorem-hypothesis instantiation, reconstruction between formulations, and target-world claim. This remains provisional pending a targeted review of philosophy of mathematical physics and scientific representation.
