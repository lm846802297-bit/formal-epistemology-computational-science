# Machine-Checked Proofs and Mature Mathematical Formalisms in Physics

## The Epistemology of Formalized Osterwalder–Schrader Reconstruction


# Abstract

Machine-checked proofs are increasingly used to formalize results in mathematical physics, yet the epistemic significance of such proofs depends on more than the validity of a derivation within a formal system. This paper examines the role of machine-checked demonstration within mature mathematical formalisms through a source-critical analysis of recent Lean formalizations of the Osterwalder–Schrader reconstruction programme. The case comprises a formal construction of the massive Gaussian free field and its OS properties, conditional reconstruction theorems from Euclidean Schwinger data to Wightman-function distributions, and a separate specification and proof-checking interface for the reconstruction statements. The analysis distinguishes formal derivability from the correspondence between a formal encoding and its source physical formalism, the instantiation of theorem hypotheses by a concrete model, and the interpretation of model results as claims about a physical target. In particular, the forward reconstruction theorem requires an arity-dependent growth condition that is not definitionally identical to the regularity condition established in the Gaussian free-field formalization. The case shows that kernel checking strengthens the deductive warrant of mathematical demonstration while leaving these further relations to be established independently. The paper develops a formalization-aware use of the denotation–demonstration–interpretation framework without proposing a new general theory of scientific representation or empirical warrant transfer.

**Keywords:** machine-checked proof; mathematical physics; scientific representation; mathematical formalism; Osterwalder–Schrader reconstruction; epistemology.


# 1. Introduction

Mathematical physics relies on formalisms whose significance extends beyond their function as notational systems. Operator methods, distribution theory, Euclidean field theory, and diagrammatic techniques organize mathematical operations while carrying interpretative and methodological commitments developed through scientific practice. They make certain inferences available, constrain admissible transformations, and provide resources through which mathematical structures acquire physical meaning (Gelfert, 2011, 2022). The increasing use of proof assistants to formalize results in these areas therefore raises a question that is at once mathematical and philosophical: what is established when a proof assistant verifies a derivation formulated within a formal representation of a physical formalism?

A proof assistant such as Lean checks whether a proposition follows from specified definitions and assumptions in a formal system. When a proof is accepted by the kernel, the result provides a strong form of deductive warrant relative to the formal language, its axioms, imported declarations, and trusted implementation. The formal turn in mathematics has clarified the significance of this kind of verification (Avigad, 2024). Related discussions of computationally opaque mathematical discovery have argued that transparent proof checking can support mathematical knowledge even when the process that generated a candidate proof is not itself transparent (Duede & Davey, 2025). These developments motivate the use of formal verification in scientific theories, where the propositions being checked are embedded in mathematical structures with physical interpretations.

The epistemological question in physics, however, is not exhausted by the validity of a formal derivation. A theorem establishes a relation among objects as defined in a formal system. A scientific claim may concern a mathematical model, a physical theory, or an intended target system. The transition between these claims requires more than proof checking. It depends on whether the formal representation adequately captures the relevant source formalism, whether a concrete model satisfies the hypotheses of the theorem, and whether the mathematical result can be interpreted as a claim about the physical system under consideration. The distinction is not a criticism of formal verification; it concerns the scope of the warrant that verification supplies.

This paper examines that scope through the formalization of the Osterwalder–Schrader (OS) reconstruction programme. Its central question is: what epistemic role does machine-checked demonstration play within a mature mathematical formalism in physics, and what further relations are required before a checked theorem can support a claim about a physical model or its target? The argument is developed as a source-critical case study of three public Lean projects: OSforGFF (Douglas et al., 2026; commit `fef84a82937dfd2bb7f672108a72da329d3ef0fd`), which formalizes a massive Gaussian free-field construction and its OS properties; OSreconstruction (Yin et al., 2026; commit `cf0904e34a7c146a9f312f601e3707dca4a87697`), which formalizes conditional reconstruction theorems between Euclidean Schwinger-function data and Wightman-function distributions; and OSreconstruction-comparator (Douglas et al., 2026; commit `93cdb40e728ba27b7b1b55efc30d57f7b43de9aa`), which supplies a separate mathematical specification and proof-checking interface for the reconstruction statements.

The philosophical framework is informed by established accounts of scientific representation and model-based reasoning. Hughes’s distinction between denotation, demonstration, and interpretation (DDI) is particularly useful because it separates the representational relation between a model and its target from the reasoning conducted within the model and the subsequent interpretation of the result (Hughes, 1997). Other accounts emphasize the pragmatic character of representation (Giere, 2004), the inferential capacities of models (Suárez, 2004), and the role of interpretation in surrogate reasoning (Contessa, 2007). Frigg and Nguyen’s DEKI account distinguishes denotation, exemplification, keying, and imputation, thereby making explicit the mapping through which properties exhibited by a model are attributed to a target (Frigg & Nguyen, 2018). These approaches differ in their theoretical commitments, but each helps explain why a valid inference within a model does not automatically establish a true claim about the model’s target.

The present problem also concerns the status of mathematical formalisms themselves. Gelfert’s analysis of mature mathematical formalisms emphasizes that they are not neutral containers for equations: they are physically interpreted systems of notation and locally applicable rules that structure scientific inference and embody theoretical and methodological commitments (Gelfert, 2011). Translating part of such a formalism into a proof assistant is consequently a selective re-articulation. Definitions, domains, operations, and assumptions are rendered explicit in a formal language, while other elements of scientific practice may remain external to the formal development. The resulting proof is rigorous relative to the formal representation; whether that representation preserves the aspects of the source formalism relevant to a particular scientific claim remains a separate question.

Several existing studies address neighboring parts of this problem. Work on mathematical proof correspondence distinguishes the adequacy of a formal statement as a representation of an informal theorem from the extent to which a formal proof tracks the reasoning of the original argument (DeDeo & Duede, 2026). Research on physics autoformalization has identified forms of semantic drift that can arise when scientific statements are translated into formal languages (Meadows et al., 2026). The epistemology of formal verification in computational science likewise emphasizes that verification has heterogeneous epistemic roles and does not replace other forms of scientific assessment (Imbert & Ardourel, 2023). In the philosophy of physics, Gelfert (2009) has already argued that mathematically rigorous relations can transmit empirical warrant between physical models. Gelfert’s work on mature mathematical formalisms further establishes that their inferential and interpretative roles are philosophically significant (Gelfert, 2011). Accordingly, this paper does not claim to introduce a general theory of scientific representation, surrogate inference, or cross-model warrant transfer.

The more specific issue is what machine checking contributes to the demonstration component of scientific model use. A checked proof can strengthen the warrant for a specified mathematical consequence and make its assumptions and inferential dependencies more auditable. It does not, by itself, establish that the formalization captures all scientifically relevant features of the source formalism, that a particular model satisfies every theorem hypothesis, or that the model is adequate for a given target and purpose. These are distinct questions, and the OS case makes their separation visible.

The original OS programme provides the mathematical context. Osterwalder and Schrader (1973) formulated axiomatic conditions relating Euclidean Green functions to relativistic Wightman theories. Their subsequent paper explicitly extended and corrected the earlier reconstruction result by introducing an additional linear growth condition (Osterwalder & Schrader, 1975). This correction is essential to the present analysis: the forward reconstruction result cannot be described accurately as following from the OS axioms E0–E4 alone. The formalized theorem must be assessed with its quantitative growth premise included.

The three Lean projects considered here formalize different components of this programme. OSforGFF constructs a massive Gaussian free field as a probability measure on a space of tempered distributions and proves that the associated generating functional satisfies the project’s OS properties. OSreconstruction formalizes conditional theorems whose forward direction takes Euclidean Schwinger data satisfying the OS conditions together with an arity-dependent growth hypothesis, and concludes the existence of a Wick-paired family of Wightman distributions. The OS-II version also establishes an output growth estimate and a uniqueness result relative to the specified Wick-pairing relation. The Comparator project states the relevant reconstruction propositions in a Mathlib-only challenge and provides an additional proof-checking interface. These are related formalization efforts, but their public interfaces do not constitute a single end-to-end proof that the particular OSforGFF model satisfies the exact growth hypothesis required by the separate OSreconstruction package.

The distinction between the projects is not merely organizational. OSforGFF formulates its result in terms of a probability measure and generating functional, whereas OSreconstruction takes Schwinger-function data on a zero-diagonal Schwartz test-function space. The projects also use different dimensional conventions and different Lean and Mathlib revisions. In particular, the regularity condition called OS1 in OSforGFF is not definitionally identical to the arity-dependent OS-II growth condition used by OSreconstruction. The OSreconstruction documentation reports concrete examples of the corrected growth condition in a private companion development; because that development is not publicly inspectable, it is treated here as an author-reported result rather than as an independently examined proof. The analysis therefore concerns what is established by the public artifacts and what additional formal interfaces would be required to compose their results.

The paper’s method is a conceptual and source-critical analysis of the exact theorem statements, assumptions, output structures, and repository records at fixed revisions, read in conjunction with the original OS papers and the philosophy of scientific representation. No new Lean implementation, numerical calculation, or independent proof replay is undertaken. The formalization results are attributed to their original authors, and claims about their verification status are restricted to the specified artifacts and the checking procedures reported by their maintainers.

The paper proceeds as follows. Section 2 examines what each OS project establishes and identifies the mathematical interfaces that remain distinct. Section 3 considers the epistemic role of machine-checked demonstration within mature mathematical formalisms, distinguishing formal derivability from model-internal claims and target-directed scientific inference. Section 4 draws together the implications for the epistemology of formalized mathematical physics.


# 2. Epistemological Analysis of Osterwalder–Schrader Reconstruction

## 2.1. The reconstruction programme and its formalization

The Osterwalder–Schrader programme concerns the relation between Euclidean field-theoretic data and relativistic quantum field theory. In the Euclidean formulation, the central objects are Schwinger functions. Under suitable conditions, these functions can be analytically continued to Wightman distributions satisfying the characteristic properties of relativistic quantum field theory, including covariance, spectral support, locality, and positivity. The original axiomatic formulation established conditions under which Euclidean Green functions determine a relativistic theory (Osterwalder & Schrader, 1973). The 1975 sequel is indispensable for stating the forward theorem precisely: it extends and corrects the earlier result and introduces the additional linear growth condition E0-prime (Osterwalder & Schrader, 1975). The reconstruction claim relevant here is therefore conditional on more than the qualitative OS conditions E0–E4.

The public Lean developments considered here formalize different parts of the reconstruction programme. OSforGFF (Douglas et al., 2026) constructs a concrete massive Gaussian free-field model and establishes its OS properties in the project’s formal setting. OSreconstruction (Yin et al., 2026) formalizes conditional theorems relating Euclidean Schwinger data to Wightman-function distributions. OSreconstruction-comparator (Douglas et al., 2026) provides a separate mathematical specification and proof-checking interface for the reconstruction propositions. The epistemic significance of these developments depends on the exact statements they establish, rather than on the general fact that all three concern the OS framework.

## 2.2. OSforGFF and the status of the concrete Euclidean model

At the revision examined here (commit `fef84a82937dfd2bb7f672108a72da329d3ef0fd`), OSforGFF constructs the massive Gaussian free field as a probability measure on the space of tempered distributions \(S'(\mathbb R^D)\). At the pinned repository revision, the project states a dimension-generic result for spacetime dimension \(D\geq 2\), together with explicit propagator instances in dimensions \(D=2,3,4,5\). Its principal theorem, gaussianFreeField_satisfies_all_OS_axioms_generic, establishes that the specified massive Gaussian free-field measure satisfies the collection of OS properties encoded by the project. These include analyticity of the generating functional, the project’s regularity condition, Euclidean invariance, reflection positivity, clustering, and an additional ergodicity property.

The mathematical content of this result is stronger than the verification of an abstract implication. A particular probability measure is constructed, and properties of its associated generating functional are established. The result therefore supports a model-internal claim about the specified massive Gaussian free-field construction. The repository reports that the relevant theorems contain no sorry and use no project-specific axioms at the pinned revision. Such statements are understood relative to the Lean environment and its foundational trust base; the present analysis does not claim to have independently rebuilt or replayed the proofs.

The scope of the theorem is determined by the definitions used in the formalization. In particular, the condition designated OS1 in OSforGFF is expressed as an exponential bound on the generating functional, together with an associated two-point integrability and convergence condition. This predicate is not definitionally identical to the arity-dependent OS-II growth condition required by OSreconstruction. The shared terminology of regularity and growth does not establish equivalence between these conditions. Any use of the OSforGFF result as an input to the OSreconstruction theorem therefore requires an explicit correspondence between the relevant formal records and a proof that the precise growth hypothesis is satisfied.

The theorem also has a specific physical scope. It establishes properties of a massive Gaussian free-field model under the assumptions encoded in the construction. It does not, solely by virtue of those properties, establish the empirical adequacy of a free-field description for an interacting system or for a particular experimental target. That distinction concerns the application of the model, not the validity of the theorem about the model.

## 2.3. OSreconstruction and the conditional character of the forward theorem

At source commit `cf0904e34a7c146a9f312f601e3707dca4a87697`, OSreconstruction formalizes forward and reverse relations between Euclidean Schwinger-function data and relativistic Wightman-function distributions. Its dimensional convention differs from that of OSforGFF: \(d\) denotes the positive spatial dimension, and spacetime has dimension \(d+1\). Thus, a four-dimensional spacetime comparison corresponds to \(D=4\) in OSforGFF and \(d=3\) in OSreconstruction.

The formal input is specified carefully. The Euclidean Schwinger functions act on a zero-diagonal Schwartz test-function space, whereas the Wightman distributions act on the full Schwartz space. The distinction is relevant to reconstruction because the passage from the Euclidean test-function domain to the full relativistic distributional domain is part of the mathematical content of the theorem. It is not merely a change in notation.

The public theorem os_to_wightman has the schematic form

\[
\mathrm{OS}(S)\land \mathrm{Growth}(S)
\Longrightarrow
\exists W\;\mathrm{WickPair}(S,W).
\]

The first antecedent is the project’s record of the OS conditions E0–E4. The second is a separate OSLinearGrowthCondition. In the formal specification, this condition is an arity-dependent estimate of the schematic form

\[
\|S_n(f)\|
\leq
\alpha\,\beta^n(n!)^\gamma
\|f\|_{ns,ns},
\]

where \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are positive, \(\gamma\) is real, and the orders of the Schwartz seminorms increase linearly with the arity \(n\). The full definition also includes normalization at arity zero and requires the estimate for every arity and every zero-diagonal test function. It is therefore a quantitative condition on the family of Schwinger distributions, rather than a generic assumption of continuity or temperedness.

The theorem concludes that there exists a Wightman-function family whose distributions are Wick-paired with the input Schwinger family. Its logical form is important: the result establishes a conditional reconstruction theorem. The existence of the Wightman family follows from the specified OS assumptions together with the exact growth condition. The proof does not establish that every Euclidean family satisfying E0–E4 alone admits the stated reconstruction.

The package also formalizes the OS-II version of the forward theorem. With the original-coordinate growth condition, the theorem yields a Wick-paired Wightman family, an output coordinate-norm growth estimate corresponding to R0-prime, and uniqueness among Wightman-function families satisfying the specified Wick-pairing relation. The uniqueness claim is consequently relative to that relation and the formalized class of Wightman families. It should not be enlarged into a claim that all possible physical interpretations or operator realizations have been uniquely determined.

The reverse theorem constructs Euclidean Schwinger data from a Wightman-function family and establishes the corresponding OS properties for the constructed data. Unlike the forward theorem, this direction does not require the OS-II growth hypothesis as an input. The asymmetry of the two directions matters: the formalized results do not amount to an unconditional biconditional between arbitrary OS data and arbitrary Wightman data.

The output of the public package is a family of Wightman distributions. The package explicitly does not claim to complete the subsequent GNS construction of a Hilbert space and operator-valued fields. Although that construction belongs to the broader mathematical context of the OS programme, it is outside the stated endpoint of this formalization. The distinction between distributional reconstruction and operator reconstruction must therefore be preserved when describing what the Lean development establishes.

The distributable OSreconstruction package does not itself expose inhabitants of its OS input record or its corrected growth-condition record. Its documentation reports that a private companion project contains examples, including the massive free field and the Proca Wick square, satisfying the relevant conditions. Since the companion project is not publicly inspectable, the present analysis records this as an author-reported result. It would be unwarranted to infer from the public package alone that no concrete model satisfies the growth condition; it would be equally unwarranted to claim that the pinned OSforGFF artifact has been formally connected to the exact input record of the pinned OSreconstruction package.

## 2.4. The Comparator and the scope of independent checking

At commit `93cdb40e728ba27b7b1b55efc30d57f7b43de9aa`, the OSreconstruction-comparator repository provides a Mathlib-only challenge specification and a corresponding solution. The challenge states the relevant reconstruction propositions using definitions that can be read independently of the production package. The solution discharges the proof obligations using the production development. The repository reports structural comparison and proof replay through Comparator and NanoDa, with the stated whitelist of Lean’s foundational axioms.

This arrangement strengthens the auditability of the formal statements and their proofs. The independently stated challenge makes the target propositions explicit outside the production package, while the replay procedure provides an additional check of the proof artifacts under the specified formal environment. The Comparator should not, however, be characterized as a second independent mathematical derivation by a separate research group. The repository describes itself as a checking interface around the production development, rather than as an independent reconstruction of the mathematics. Nor does the procedure provide empirical validation of the physical theory.

The Comparator documentation also discloses conventions that delimit the exact propositions under examination. These include an additional condition on the first imaginary coordinate in the forward tube, locality formulated for adjacent transpositions, reflection positivity expressed through a totalized coercion onto zero-diagonal test functions, and the use of growth as a forward hypothesis rather than a conclusion of the reverse construction. Such conventions are part of the mathematical specification being checked. Accordingly, the epistemic assessment must concern the formalized propositions as stated, rather than a broader textbook formulation that may suppress these choices.

## 2.5. The relation between the formal developments

The three projects cannot be combined into a single proof chain merely because they concern the same reconstruction programme. Their relation involves several distinct mathematical interfaces.

First, the formal records differ. OSforGFF formulates its result through a probability measure and its generating functional. OSreconstruction takes a family of Schwinger distributions on a zero-diagonal Schwartz test-function space. To use the output of the former as the input of the latter, one needs a map between these records that preserves the relevant mathematical structure.

Second, the regularity assumptions differ. The OS1 condition in OSforGFF is not definitionally the same as the arity-dependent OS-II growth condition in OSreconstruction. Direct application of the reconstruction theorem therefore requires either a proof that the concrete model satisfies the latter condition or an explicit theorem relating the two formal predicates. The fact that both conditions concern growth does not supply such a proof.

Third, the dimensional conventions must be translated. OSforGFF uses \(D\) for spacetime dimension, whereas OSreconstruction uses \(d\) for spatial dimension and \(d+1\) for spacetime. The four-dimensional spacetime case is thus represented by \(D=4\) in the former project and \(d=3\) in the latter. Finally, the projects use different Lean and Mathlib revisions. Their respective theorems are established in their own pinned environments; direct composition is not supplied by the separate theorem statements.

The public artifacts inspected do not expose a single end-to-end theorem that takes the specific OSforGFF construction and proves that it satisfies the exact OSreconstruction growth input at the pinned revisions. This is a claim about the public interface examined here, not a claim that the required mathematical relation is impossible or that no such result exists in the private companion development reported by the authors. The distinction is important because the epistemological issue concerns the warrant actually made available by the formal artifacts, not the mathematical possibility of extending them.

## 2.6. Denotation, demonstration, and interpretation in the OS case

The DDI distinction provides a useful structure for assessing these results. At the level of denotation, OSforGFF specifies a mathematical probability model: the massive Gaussian free field on a space of tempered distributions, with covariance determined by the propagator construction. OSreconstruction, by contrast, takes Euclidean Schwinger-function data on a specified test-function space. The physical interpretation of these objects is supplied by the OS framework, but the formal relation between the two records requires an explicit map. A common physical designation does not establish formal equivalence.

At the level of demonstration, the Lean projects establish different mathematical claims. OSforGFF proves the bundled OS properties for its specified Gaussian free-field construction. OSreconstruction proves conditional reconstruction theorems from its exact OS and growth hypotheses. The Comparator adds a separate statement and proof-checking interface for the reconstruction propositions. Machine checking contributes most directly at this level: it makes the mathematical demonstration explicit, subjects proof objects to kernel checking, and permits the assumptions and theorem endpoints to be examined. Its contribution is substantial, but its object is the formal proposition as encoded.

At the level of interpretation, the mathematical structures are understood through the physical relation between Euclidean Schwinger functions, reflection positivity, analytic continuation, and relativistic Wightman distributions. The formalized reconstruction result has a clear place within axiomatic quantum field theory. Nevertheless, the distributional endpoint should not be conflated with the subsequent construction of a Hilbert space and operator-valued fields, which the public package does not claim to complete.

The distinction becomes sharper when a model is used to make a target-directed claim. The OS properties of the massive Gaussian free field establish mathematical facts about that model. Whether the model is used as an exact representation of a free theory, as an idealization, or as a component of an approximation to a more complex system depends on the scientific purpose and the relevant model-to-target relation. The machine-checked theorem does not determine that purpose or establish empirical adequacy for an interacting system.

## 2.7. The epistemic content of the formalized results

The OS case supports three different kinds of claim. The first is a claim about a formal derivation: a proposition follows from specified assumptions in a specified formal system. The second is a model-internal claim: the interpreted mathematical proposition holds of a concrete model, provided that the model satisfies every hypothesis in the formal theorem. The third is a target-directed scientific claim: a property of the model warrants an inference about a physical system for a specified purpose. These claims are related, but they are not interchangeable.

The formal artifacts establish strong results at the first level and, in the case of OSforGFF, a concrete model-internal result at the second. The forward OSreconstruction theorem establishes a conditional mathematical relation, but its application to a particular model requires an instance of the exact growth hypothesis. A target-directed claim requires a further account of the model’s representational role and the evidence appropriate to the intended application. This structure does not diminish the mathematical achievements of the formalizations. It specifies the different kinds of knowledge to which those achievements can contribute.


# 3. Discussion: Machine-Checked Demonstration and Scientific Warrant

The OS case illustrates both the epistemic strength and the scope of machine-checked proof in mathematical physics. A proof assistant does not merely provide an additional informal endorsement of a derivation. It checks a proof object against a formal proposition in a specified environment. When the definitions, assumptions, imports, and foundational trust base are explicit, the resulting warrant is deductive and auditable. If the kernel accepts a proof of

\[
\Gamma\vdash_{\mathcal L}\varphi,
\]

then, relative to the soundness of the formal system and its declared trust base, \(\varphi\) follows from \(\Gamma\) as represented in the language \(\mathcal L\). The epistemic force of this result is considerable: the proof is not accepted because its informal presentation appears persuasive, but because the formal derivation satisfies the system’s rules.

The warrant is nevertheless local to the proposition and assumptions that have been formalized. Kernel acceptance does not independently establish that the assumptions express the intended physical conditions, that the formal proposition captures the intended scientific claim, or that the formal objects have the physical interpretation assigned to them. This qualification does not weaken the mathematical theorem. It identifies the relation between proof validity and the wider epistemic tasks involved in scientific reasoning.

The Comparator layer in the OS case illustrates an additional form of scrutiny. By stating the reconstruction propositions in a Mathlib-only challenge and checking the corresponding solution through a separate replay procedure, the project makes the formal target more accessible to inspection and provides an additional check on the production proof. The procedure strengthens confidence in the specified mathematical statement and its proof artifact. It does not establish empirical adequacy, nor does it constitute a second independent derivation of the OS reconstruction theorem. The epistemic contribution of this layer is therefore best understood as an increase in the auditability of formal demonstration.

The case also shows why formalization should be understood as a selective re-articulation of a mature physical formalism. As Gelfert (2011, 2022) emphasizes, mathematical formalisms in scientific practice carry local rules, conventions, and theoretical commitments that shape the inferences available to practitioners. A proof assistant renders selected parts of this structure explicit as types, definitions, hypotheses, and theorems. Other aspects may remain in explanatory prose or in the interpretative practice through which the formalism is used. This selection is not intrinsically defective; it is a condition of formalization. The epistemic question is whether the selected formal representation is adequate for the claim that the proof is subsequently used to support.

The difference between the OS1 condition in OSforGFF and the OS-II growth condition in OSreconstruction provides a concrete example. Both predicates concern regularity or growth, but their mathematical definitions and roles differ. The former is expressed as a bound on the generating functional, whereas the latter is an arity-dependent estimate on the family of Schwinger distributions. A theorem establishing one condition cannot be used as evidence for the other without a further mathematical argument. Formalization makes this distinction explicit: similarly named assumptions are represented by distinct predicates, and the reconstruction theorem requires the particular predicate appearing in its statement.

This point is closely related to the distinction between proving a conditional theorem and establishing that a particular model satisfies its antecedent. The forward reconstruction theorem has the form

\[
\mathrm{OS}(S)\land\mathrm{Growth}(S)
\Longrightarrow
\exists W\;\mathrm{WickPair}(S,W).
\]

The theorem establishes a general relation between Euclidean data satisfying the stated conditions and a corresponding Wightman-function family. To infer that a particular model has such a reconstruction, one must establish that its Schwinger data satisfy both antecedents in the exact formal sense used by the theorem. The proof of the implication does not itself supply this instance. In the public OSreconstruction package, the input records are not inhabited within the distributable package; the authors report examples in a private companion development. The public OSforGFF result, in turn, does not automatically provide the precise growth-condition instance required by the separate reconstruction package. The distinction is a standard one in mathematical reasoning, but its epistemic significance becomes especially clear when the assumptions are explicit and machine-checked.

A further transition is required when a model-internal result is used to make a claim about a physical target. The DDI account distinguishes denotation, demonstration, and interpretation (Hughes, 1997). The OS case shows that machine checking can strengthen demonstration while leaving the other components to be assessed. The relation between a mathematical model and its target is not secured merely by the validity of deductions carried out within the model. As Suárez (2004) and Contessa (2007) emphasize in different ways, models support surrogate reasoning, but the validity of an inference within or through a model does not by itself guarantee that the inference is sound for the target. Frigg and Nguyen’s (2018) account similarly makes explicit the mapping through which properties exhibited by a model are keyed to, and imputed to, a target.

The physical significance of the OS reconstruction result is not in question. The relation between Euclidean Schwinger functions and relativistic Wightman distributions is central to axiomatic quantum field theory. The point is that the formal theorem establishes this relation under its specified mathematical assumptions. Its use in a claim about a particular physical system additionally depends on how the model is intended to represent that system and on whether the relevant features are preserved in the application. A massive Gaussian free field can be an exact mathematical model of a free field under its assumptions; that fact does not make it an empirically adequate description of an interacting system. The latter assessment depends on the target and the purpose of the model, as emphasized by adequacy-for-purpose approaches to model evaluation (Parker, 2020).

This analysis is consistent with, and should be distinguished from, Gelfert’s account of cross-model warrant transfer. Gelfert (2009) argues that mathematically rigorous relations can transmit empirical warrant between physical models, as illustrated by the relation between Hubbard and Heisenberg models. The present argument does not challenge this possibility or claim it as a new result. Rather, it specifies the role that machine checking could play within such a transfer: a proof assistant can strengthen the deductive warrant for the mathematical relation connecting models, while the empirical warrant of the source model and the physical relevance of the relation remain separate requirements. Machine checking can therefore improve one component of a warrant-transfer argument without generating empirical warrant independently.

Nor should the analysis imply that every mathematically valuable result must culminate in an empirical claim about an actual target. Models may function as epistemic artifacts through which scientists investigate possible structures, mechanisms, and modal relations (Knuuttila, 2021). A machine-checked theorem about such a model can contribute mathematical or modal knowledge even when no direct target-directed inference is intended. The appropriate epistemological assessment is therefore claim-sensitive: the warrant required for a formal theorem, a model-internal result, an explanatory claim, and an empirical claim need not be identical.

The discussion yields a differentiated account of the epistemic contribution of machine-checked proof. At the level of formal derivation, kernel checking provides deductive warrant relative to the declared formal system and trust base. At the level of a concrete model, the formal statement must correspond to the intended mathematical claim and the model must satisfy all theorem hypotheses. At the level of a physical target, the inference must be justified through an appropriate representational and interpretative relation, assessed relative to the scientific purpose and relevant evidence. Explanatory understanding is related to these forms of warrant but is not reducible to proof validity.

The resulting picture is not a linear sequence in which a formal theorem is automatically converted into an empirical conclusion. It is a set of related but distinguishable epistemic relations: formal derivation within a specified system, realization of the theorem’s hypotheses in a model, interpretation of the model’s structures, and—where a target-directed claim is intended—an assessment of the model’s relevance to that target and purpose. The OS formalizations make these distinctions unusually visible because their theorem statements and interfaces are explicit. The same distinctions apply more broadly wherever mathematical formalisms are used to support scientific inference.


# 4. Conclusion

This paper has examined what a machine-checked proof establishes when the formal system being verified is a selective formalization of a mathematical formalism used in physics. The Osterwalder–Schrader case indicates that the answer must be stated at more than one level. Kernel checking provides deductive warrant for a proposition relative to its formal definitions, assumptions, imports, and trust base. It does not, by itself, establish that the formal proposition captures every scientifically relevant feature of the source formalism, that a particular model satisfies the hypotheses of a conditional theorem, or that a result about the model warrants a claim about a physical target.

The three formalization projects illustrate these distinctions. OSforGFF establishes, within its formal setting, that the specified massive Gaussian free-field construction satisfies the project’s OS properties. OSreconstruction proves conditional forward and reverse reconstruction results, with the forward direction requiring the OS assumptions together with a separate arity-dependent growth condition. The Comparator project supplies an additional specification and proof-checking interface for the reconstruction statements. These results are mathematically substantial, but their scopes are not interchangeable. In particular, the OS1 regularity condition in OSforGFF is not definitionally identical to the growth condition required by OSreconstruction, and the public artifacts examined do not expose a single end-to-end theorem instantiating the latter condition from the former construction. The reconstruction package’s stated endpoint is a family of Wightman distributions, rather than a full GNS construction of a Hilbert space and operator-valued fields.

The philosophical significance of these distinctions can be expressed through the DDI framework. Machine checking strengthens demonstration: it makes selected assumptions and inferential steps explicit and subjects the proof object to formal checking. Denotation and interpretation remain separate. The formalization must correspond adequately to the relevant source formalism, and a model-internal result must be interpreted before it can support a target-directed inference. Where the scientific claim concerns an actual physical system, the adequacy of that inference depends on the representational relation, the purpose of the model, and the relevant theoretical or empirical evidence. This conclusion is consistent with existing accounts of surrogate reasoning and model evaluation, and it complements analyses of how rigorous mathematical relations can transmit empirical warrant between physical models (Gelfert, 2009).

The argument does not imply that every formal result must culminate in an empirical claim. A machine-checked theorem may establish mathematical or modal knowledge about a model that functions as an epistemic artifact, without being used to represent a particular actual system. What matters is that the epistemic claim be identified correctly and assessed by the standards appropriate to it. Formal derivability, model-internal truth, explanatory understanding, and target-directed warrant are related achievements, but none should be treated as a substitute for the others.

The contribution of machine-checked proof in mathematical physics is therefore both significant and delimited. It strengthens the deductive warrant and auditability of demonstration within a formalized mathematical structure. It does not eliminate the interpretative and representational work through which that structure is connected to scientific practice and, where intended, to the physical world. The OS case makes this division of epistemic labour explicit: the proof assistant can certify what follows from the formalized assumptions, while the scientific significance of those assumptions, their realization in a model, and the scope of the resulting physical claims remain matters for mathematical and philosophical analysis.
