# Abstract

Machine-checked proofs establish consequences relative to the definitions and assumptions of a formal system, but scientific reasoning may require results to pass between different formal representations of a physical theory. This paper asks when such a transfer is warranted, using three Lean developments in the Osterwalder–Schrader (OS) programme. OSforGFF constructs a massive Gaussian free field in a Glimm–Jaffe probability-measure formulation; OSreconstruction proves conditional distributional reconstruction theorems from zero-diagonal Schwinger data; and the OSreconstruction Comparator checks an audit challenge against production results through explicit record bridges. The challenge reproduces the production package's own Mathlib-only audit definitions, so the Comparator is a separately elaborated checking interface, not an independently authored physical specification or a second mathematical derivation. It provides a limited positive case of record-level interface warrant: the bridges preserve the Schwinger family, growth assumptions, and theorem conclusions. By contrast, the inspected public artifacts do not expose an end-to-end instantiation of the OSreconstruction input from the particular OSforGFF construction. The analysis distinguishes local proof warrant, source-formalism correspondence, record-level interface warrant, model instantiation, and target-directed inference. It uses *inter-formalization warrant* as a bounded analytical label for the conditions under which a result may be reused across formal representations, not as a new universal theory or proof-transfer protocol.

**Keywords:** machine-checked proof; mathematical physics; formalization; scientific representation; Osterwalder–Schrader reconstruction; epistemology.
