# Abstract

Machine-checked proofs can establish mathematical consequences with high deductive reliability, but the scientific use of such results often involves more than one formal representation of a physical theory. This paper asks under what conditions a theorem proved in one formalization can warrant an inference in another. The question is examined through three public Lean developments associated with Osterwalder–Schrader reconstruction: OSforGFF, which constructs a massive Gaussian free field in a Glimm–Jaffe probability-measure formulation; OSreconstruction, which proves conditional distributional reconstruction theorems from zero-diagonal Euclidean Schwinger data; and a Comparator project that checks the reconstruction statements through an independently elaborated specification and explicit record-equivalence bridges. The analysis distinguishes local proof warrant, correspondence between a source formalism and its encoding, inter-formalization warrant, hypothesis instantiation, and target-directed scientific inference. The case shows both a positive instance of relative interface warrant, where the Comparator bridges preserve the Schwinger family and growth assumptions without independently validating the source specification's physical adequacy, and a missing public interface, since the OSforGFF construction is not formally instantiated as an input to the OSreconstruction package at the examined revisions. The paper uses inter-formalization warrant as an epistemological label for this interface requirement, not as a new proof-transfer protocol; scientific representation and empirical warrant remain further, distinct questions.

**Keywords:** machine-checked proof; mathematical physics; formalization; scientific representation; Osterwalder–Schrader reconstruction; epistemology.
